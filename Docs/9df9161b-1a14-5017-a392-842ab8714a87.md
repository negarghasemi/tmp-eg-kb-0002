# Electric and gas ranges (cooking products)

*November 2024*

## Technical bulletin on amending the standards

In April 2024, the Office of Energy Efficiency at Natural Resources Canada (NRCan) updated its [Forward Regulatory Plan](https://www.nrcan.gc.ca/energy/energy-efficiency/energy-efficiency-regulations/forward-regulatory-plan/21709) which identifies its intention to amend Canadas *Energy Efficiency Regulations* (the Regulations) to harmonize the scope and the energy efficiency and testing standards for electric and gas ranges with those of the United States for conventional cooking products.

NRCan is considering:
*   Consolidating the energy-using product names for **electric** and **gas ranges** under **cooking products** to better harmonize with the United States.
*   Removing current energy efficiency standards for **electric coil cooktops** and for **electric oven on-mode** to harmonize with the United States. A new prescriptive standard would be adopted for ovens to also harmonize with the United States. This approach would have negligible energy implications, while facilitating trade and improving the availability and variety of efficient products for Canadians.
*   Updating the energy efficiency standard for **gas cooktops** to harmonize with the United States, introducing an annual energy consumption requirement that would replace the current prescriptive requirement of no standing pilot.

This technical bulletin initiates the consultation process to collect stakeholder views. Input will inform the development of future amendments. NRCan will also conduct a cost-benefit analysis.

The Government of Canada intends to pre-publish a regulatory proposal in the *Canada Gazette*, Part I, in **2025**.

## Background

*   In Canada, household electric and gas ranges manufactured as of **February 3, 1995**, are subject to the Regulations. Induction cooking products are not currently regulated.
*   Energy efficiency standards for cooking products aim to save energy, reduce costs, decrease energy-related greenhouse gas emissions, and encourage efficient product designs.
*   NRCan and the U.S. Department of Energy (U.S. DOE) signed a Memorandum of Understanding to collaborate on energy efficiency and testing standards.
*   On **February 14, 2024**, the U.S. DOE published a Direct Final Rule strengthening energy efficiency standards for gas, induction, and electric resistance cooktops (excluding electric coil cooktops). Compliance is required on or after **January 31, 2028**.
*   On **August 8, 2022**, the U.S. DOE published a Final Rule for the testing standard of cooking products, updated with corrections on February 7, 2023. Use of this testing standard is mandatory in the U.S. since **February 20, 2023**.
*   NRCan intends to harmonize with U.S. DOE standards, scope, testing standard, and compliance dates.
*   The Government of Canada is committed to improving energy efficiency standards to support the goal of **net-zero emissions by 2050**.

## Product description

Household electric and gas ranges are currently defined separately. NRCan intends to consolidate them under a single subdivision using U.S. harmonized definitions:

*   **Electric range**: A household electric range, excluding portable 120V ranges or microwave ovens.
*   **Gas range**: A household propane or natural gas range with an electrical power source, used for food preparation, providing surface, oven, or broiling cooking.
*   **Range**: Currently includes gas or electric cooktops (non-induction), gas or electric ovens (e.g., wall oven), or combined cooking products.

**New terms considered for the Regulations:**

*   **Cooking product**: A cooktop, an oven, or a combined cooking product.
*   **Cooktop**: A household cooking appliance with a horizontal surface containing one or more surface units utilizing gas flame, electric resistance heating, or electric inductive heating. Includes cooktop components of combined cooking products.
*   **Oven**: A household cooking appliance with one or more compartments for cooking/heating food by gas flame or electric resistance heating. Includes oven components of combined cooking products. Excludes microwave ovens or portable/countertop electric resistance ovens designed for 120 volts.
*   **Combined cooking product**: A household cooking appliance combining a cooking product with other appliance functionality (which may or may not include another cooking product).

## Testing standard

NRCan is considering incorporating by reference an NRCan technical standards document (as amended from time to time) that would reproduce:

*   **Appendix I1 to Subpart B, Part 430 of Title 10** to the U.S. Code of Federal Regulations, entitled *Uniform Test Method for Measuring the Energy Consumption of Conventional Cooking Products*.

## Energy efficiency standards

The energy efficiency standards under consideration for cooking products would be harmonized with U.S. subparagraphs 430.32(j)(1)(iii) and 430.32(j)(2)(ii) of Subpart C, Part 430 of Title 10 to the United States *Code of Federal Regulations*, as amended from time to time:

*   **Electric smooth element cooktops** (resistance and induction) have a maximum **Integrated Annual Energy Consumption (IAEC)** of **207 kWh/year**.
*   **Gas cooktops** have a maximum **IAEC** of **519 kWh/year** or **1770 kBtu/year**.
*   **Oven control systems** shall not include a **linear power supply**.

 *IAEC excludes any downdraft venting system energy consumption.*

These standards would be included in an NRCan technical standards document referenced by the Regulations, as amended from time to time.

## Important dates

*   **Coming into force**: Six months after the date of publication in the *Canada Gazette*, Part II.
*   **Application of updated standards**: On or after **January 31, 2028**, to harmonize with the U.S. compliance date.
*   **Voluntary compliance**: Dealers could voluntarily comply with the changes upon their publication in the *Canada Gazette*, Part II.

## Regulatory pathway

NRCan is considering amending the Regulations through Governor in Council amendment, followed by pre-publication in the *Canada Gazette*, Part I, for public comment.

## Labelling requirements

*   An EnerGuide label is currently required on electric ranges.
*   NRCan is considering **phasing out the current labelling requirement** for these products to harmonize with the United States, which does not require labelling for cooking products.
*   NRCan would reconsider an EnerGuide label if the U.S. Federal Trade Commission implements a similar requirement.

## Verification requirements

No change is being considered in regard to verification requirements.

## Reporting requirements

### Energy efficiency report

The energy efficiency report required for cooking products would include the following product specific information:

*   Whether the cooking product is: **a cooktop, oven(s), or combined cooking product**.
*   If a combined cooking product, its components: **cooktop, oven, and/or microwave oven**.
*   The fuel(s) used: **electricity, gas, or both**.
*   If a cooktop or combined cooking product with a cooktop component, the cooktop surface technology: **resistive coil, resistive smooth, or induction**.
*   If an oven or combined cooking product with an oven component: whether it includes a **linear power supply**.
*   The IAEC of the cooking product: in **kWh/year**, or in **kBtu/year** for gas cooktops, if applicable.

This report must be submitted by the dealer to NRCan **before** the product is imported into Canada or traded inter-provincially for the first time.

### Import report

No change is being considered in regard to import reporting.

## Comments invited

NRCan is specifically interested in feedback on:

*   The implications of removing energy efficiency standards for **electric coil cooktops**.
*   The implications of removing **on-mode energy efficiency standards for electric ovens**.
*   The implications of removing the **EnerGuide label for ranges** (i.e. electric cooktops and ovens).
