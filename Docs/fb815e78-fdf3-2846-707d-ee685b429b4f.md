# Air Cleaners

**May 2024**

## Technical Bulletin on Developing the Standards

**Webinar**: Wednesday, May 29, 1:00-2:00 pm EDT

In April 2024, the Office of Energy Efficiency at Natural Resources Canada (NRCan) updated its [Forward Regulatory Plan](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/node/21709#msdynttrid=gPrvBKD4QnHLNydHNDucp-nQokQOZTaBRjIp6V7eiWk), identifying its intention to amend Canadas *Energy Efficiency Regulations*. This amendment would introduce air cleaners as a new energy-using product, incorporating energy efficiency and testing standards harmonized with the United States.

This technical bulletin initiates the consultation process to collect stakeholder views on the proposed requirements. Input will inform the development of Amendment 19. NRCan will also conduct a cost-benefit analysis using Canadian market data to assess economic and environmental impacts. The Government of Canada plans to pre-publish a regulatory proposal in the *Canada Gazette*, Part I, in 2025.

## Background

Air cleaners are currently not defined as an energy-using product in the Regulations. Internal analysis indicates significant energy use by air cleaners in Canada, with consumer ownership and shipments increasing due to the COVID-19 pandemic (80% increase from 2019 to 2020) and concerns over air quality (wildfires, allergens).

Energy efficiency standards for air cleaners aim to:
- Save energy and reduce energy costs for Canadians.
- Reduce energy-related greenhouse gas emissions.
- Encourage manufacturers to adopt more efficient product designs.

NRCan and the U.S. Department of Energy (U.S. DOE) signed a [Memorandum of Understanding](https://nrcan.canada.ca/energy/resources/international-energy-cooperation/memorandum-understanding/23749) in 2021 to collaborate on energy efficiency and testing standards.

On April 6, 2023, the U.S. DOE published a [direct final rule](https://www.regulations.gov/document/EERE-2021-BT-TP-0036-0036) to establish new energy efficiency standards for air cleaners. The U.S. testing standard is detailed in [Appendix FF](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-10/part-430/appendix-Appendix FF to Subpart B of Part 430) to Subpart B of Part 430, Title 10 of the U.S. *Code of Federal Regulations*, titled *Uniform Test Method for Measuring the Energy Consumption of Air Cleaners*.

The U.S. DOE rule established two tiers for conventional room air cleaners:
- **Tier 1 standards**: Effective December 31, 2023.
- **Tier 2 standards**: More stringent, effective December 31, 2025.

NRCan is considering harmonizing with the **U.S. Tier 2 energy efficiency standards** for products manufactured on or after December 31, 2025.

## Product Description

**Air cleaner**: An electrically-powered, self-contained, mechanically encased assembly that improves indoor air quality by removing, destroying or deactivating particulates, VOC (volatile organic compounds), and/or microorganisms from the air.

*Exclusions*: Does not include a central air conditioner, room air conditioner, portable air conditioner, dehumidifier, furnace, or product that operates solely by means of ultraviolet light without a fan for air circulation.

This definition is harmonized with section 430.2 to Subpart A of Part 430 of Title 10 to the U.S. *Code of Federal Regulations* ([10 CFR 430.2 Air cleaner](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-10/part-430/section-430.2#p-430.2(Air%20cleaner))). 

### Testing Standard

The testing standard under consideration is:
- [Appendix FF](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-10/part-430/appendix-Appendix FF to Subpart B of Part 430) to Subpart B of Part 430, Title 10 of the U.S. *Code of Federal Regulations*, entitled *Uniform Test Method for Measuring the Energy Consumption of Air Cleaners*.

NRCan intends to reproduce this testing standard in an NRCan Technical Standards Document (TSD) referenced by the Regulations.

### Energy Efficiency Standard

The energy efficiency standards under consideration for air cleaners (harmonizing with U.S. Code of Federal Regulations) are summarized below:

| Product Class (CADR)            | Integrated Energy Factor (PM. CADR/W) |
|---------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| **CADR [cfm]** | **CADR [m/h]** | **[cfm/W]** | **[m/Wh]** |
| 10  PM. CADR < 100          | 17  PM. CADR < 170   | ** 1.9**   | ** 3.22**  |
| 100  PM. CADR < 150         | 170  PM. CADR < 255  | ** 2.4**   | ** 4.07**  |
| PM. CADR  150              | PM. CADR  255       | ** 2.9**   | ** 4.92**  |

*Notes:*
- **Clean Air Delivery Rate (CADR)**: Measure of contaminant-free air delivery by an air cleaner, expressed in cubic feet per minute (cfm) or cubic metres per hour (m/h).
- **CADR calculation**: Rate of contaminant reduction in the test chamber (air cleaner on) minus natural decay rate (air cleaner off), multiplied by test chamber volume.
- **PM.**: Particulate matter with an aerodynamic diameter  2.5 micrometers (m) as measured by a reference method.

### Energy efficiency standards table description

The table above outlines the energy efficiency standards for air cleaners, categorized by product class based on Clean Air Delivery Rate (CADR). The Integrated Energy Factor (PM. CADR/W) is the energy efficiency standard each product class must meet. PM. CADR refers to particulate matter with an aerodynamic diameter less than or equal to 2.5 micrometers. For example:
- For air cleaners with a CADR between 10 cfm and 100 cfm (or 17 m/h and 170 m/h), the Integrated Energy Factor must be ** 1.9 cfm/W** (or ** 3.22 m/Wh**).
- For air cleaners with a CADR between 100 cfm and 150 cfm (or 170 m/h and 255 m/h), the Integrated Energy Factor must be ** 2.4 cfm/W** (or ** 4.07 m/Wh**).
- For air cleaners with a CADR  150 cfm (or  255 m/h), the Integrated Energy Factor must be ** 2.9 cfm/W** (or ** 4.92 m/Wh**).

## Important Dates

- **Effective Date of Modifications**: Six months after the date of publication in the *Canada Gazette*, Part II.
- **Application of New Standards**: To air cleaners manufactured on or after **December 31, 2025**.

### Regulatory Pathway

NRCan is considering amending the Regulations through Governor in Council amendment. Proposed changes will be pre-published in the *Canada Gazette*, Part I, followed by a public comment period. Final publication in the *Canada Gazette*, Part II, typically occurs within a year of the initial public comment period, subject to decision-making authorities under the *Energy Efficiency Act*.

## Labelling Requirements

NRCan is considering introducing **EnerGuide labelling requirements** for air cleaners. The primary measurement on the EnerGuide label would be the **Clean Air Delivery Rate (CADR)/Watt (CADR/W)**. The intent is for an EnerGuide label to be attached to every new air cleaner sold in Canada.

## Verification Requirements

Air cleaners would require a **third-party verification mark** indicating compliance with energy efficiency standards and verification of reported information. This mark would be from a Standards Council of Canada accredited certification body operating an energy efficiency certification program for the product.

## Reporting Requirements

### Energy Efficiency Report

The energy efficiency report for air cleaners would include:
- **Clean Air Delivery Rate with particulate diameter of 2.5 microns (PM. CADR)**, expressed in cfm or m/h.
- **Integrated Energy Factor (PM. CADR/W)**, expressed in cfm/W or m/Wh.

This report must be submitted by the dealer to NRCan **before** the product is first imported into Canada or traded inter-provincially.

### Import Report

A dealer importing these products into Canada would include the following information on the customs release document:
- **Name of product**: Air cleaner
- **Model number**
- **Brand name**
- **Address of the dealer importing the product**
- **Purpose for which the product is being imported** (e.g., for sale/lease in Canada without modification, for sale/lease after modification to comply, or for use as a component in an exported product).
