# Best Practices in Heat Pumps: Energy Efficiency Insights

This document summarizes best practices in heat pump sizing, selection, and installation for various property types, highlighting strategies for cost-effectiveness, comfort, and greenhouse gas (GHG) savings.

## Property Energy Management Cases

### 1. Net Zero All Electric Homes  A Builders Journey

*   **Property Type**: New custom homes (Watercolour Westport, Ontario).
*   **Objective**: Build net-zero energy ready, all-electric homes.
*   **Key Challenges**: Absence of natural gas, rising material/trade costs, regulatory hurdles, need for trade training, and homeowner education on new systems.
*   **Energy Efficiency Measures Implemented**:
    *   **Super Insulation**: Continuous spray foam on walls and under basement slab, insulation wrapping for thermal bridging, triple-glazed windows.
    *   **Super Tight Building Envelope**: Achieved through advanced sealing techniques.
    *   **Super Ventilation**: Integration of efficient HVAC systems.
*   **Outcomes**: Homes achieved excellent EnerGuide ratings, high comfort levels (no drafts), and a **50% reduction in energy bills** for operation.
*   **Key Learning**: Adopting net-zero strategies, while challenging initially, positions builders ahead of future building codes and offers significant long-term benefits.

### 2. Best Practices for Sizing Supplementary Heating Systems in Heat Pump Retrofits

*   **Property Type**: 11 townhouses (retrofit project).
*   **Objective**: Maximize energy, GHG, and cost savings by minimizing reliance on backup electric heat and avoiding expensive electrical panel upgrades.
*   **Key Challenge**: Over-reliance on backup heat can negate savings; electrical panel upgrades can cost **$3,000 to $10,000**.
*   **Energy Efficiency Measures Implemented**:
    *   **Envelope Improvements First**: Upgrading the building envelope significantly reduced the heating load (by **70%** in this case).
    *   **Right-Sizing Heat Pumps**: Allowed for the installation of smaller heat pumps and a smaller **3KW supplementary backup heater**, anticipated to operate only **5%** of the heating season.
*   **Outcomes**: Avoided costly electrical panel upgrades, leading to substantial energy, GHG, and cost savings.
*   **Key Learning**: Prioritizing building envelope improvements enables the installation of smaller, more efficient heat pump systems, reducing overall project costs and optimizing performance.

### 3. Heat Pump Controls as Part of Net-Zero Strategies for Colleges and Universities

*   **Property Type**: Fanshawe Colleges Kestrel Court (66 townhouses for students).
*   **Objective**: Reduce greenhouse gas emissions by **30% by 2030** and **80% by 2050**, moving towards net-zero targets.
*   **Energy Efficiency Measures Implemented**:
    *   **Envelope First Approach**: Air sealing, insulation, and window upgrades.
    *   **Right-Sizing HVAC**: Equipment sized appropriately for the improved building shell.
    *   **Advanced Control Strategies**: Utilizing Thermal Balance Point and Economic Balance Point to ensure efficient heat pump operation and minimize supplementary natural gas heating.
*   **Outcomes**: Significant cost and GHG reductions, maintaining thermal and economic balance points effectively.
*   **Key Learning**: Proper control strategies are crucial for optimizing heat pump efficiency and achieving ambitious net-zero targets in institutional settings.

### 4. Reducing Heating Loads to Optimize Heat Pump Retrofits

*   **Property Type**: 11 townhouse units at Fanshawe College.
*   **Initial State**: High air leakage (5 air changes per hour).
*   **Objective**: Improve comfort and reduce GHG emissions.
*   **Energy Efficiency Measures Implemented (Prioritized for ROI)**:
    1.  **Air Sealing**: Identified as having the quickest return on investment.
    2.  **Attic Insulation**.
    3.  **Window Upgrades**: High-performance, low-emissivity, triple-glazed units.
    4.  **Basement and Wall Insulation**.
    *   Exterior Finish Insulation System (EFIS) applied to the building envelope.
    *   Heat Recovery Ventilators (HRV) installed.
    *   Mechanical systems right-sized based on CSA Standard F280-12 heat loss/gain calculations.
*   **Outcomes**: Achieved a **70% reduction in energy and GHG emissions**, allowing for much smaller and more efficient heat pump and backup systems. Net Zero Energy target achieved.
*   **Key Learning**: A methodical, envelope-first approach significantly reduces heating loads, leading to cost-effective and highly efficient heat pump retrofits.

### 5. Using NRCans ASHP Sizing and Selection Tool to Drive Heat Pump Sales

*   **Property Type**: University of Waterloo Student Housing co-op (94 multi-residential units).
*   **Initial State**: Electric baseboard heating, desire for AC.
*   **Tool Utilized**: NRCans Air-source Heat Pump (ASHP) Sizing and Selection Guide.
*   **Process**: The tool facilitated a data-driven approach to:
    *   Define ASHP configuration, indoor unit types, heating/cooling loads, and target capacity.
    *   Identify and shortlist suitable equipment.
    *   Select optimal system control strategies for cost, GHG, and energy savings.
    *   Evaluate backup heating requirements.
*   **Outcomes**: The tool helped demonstrate that heat pumps were a superior choice over standard AC, provided a competitive advantage in sales, and resulted in significant GHG reductions.
*   **Key Learning**: Data-driven tools like NRCan's ASHP guide empower HVAC professionals to make objective comparisons and present compelling business cases for heat pump retrofits.

### 6. Net-Zero Transition: Electrify Your Home with Heat Pumps

*   **Property Type**: 22-year-old home.
*   **Objective**: Decarbonize, eliminate GHG, better load management, achieve net-zero.
*   **Initial State**: Natural gas heating, electric water heater, EV charger, 100 amp electrical panel.
*   **Key Challenge**: Electrification can trigger expensive electrical panel upgrades (**$3,000 - $12,000**) and strain the grid.
*   **Energy Efficiency Measures Implemented**:
    *   Replaced gas stove with induction cooktop.
    *   Right-sized heat pump (2.5-ton for a 30,000 BTU/hr load) to avoid immediate panel upgrade.
    *   Used a smaller electric backup heater instead of full-load resistance heaters.
    *   **Strategic Upgrade**: Ultimately upgraded to a 200 amp service with a split panel for future needs (greenhouse, solar, battery storage).
*   **Outcomes**: Strategic planning allowed for heat pump installation without immediate panel upgrade. The later 200 amp upgrade provided long-term resiliency and load management options.
*   **Key Learning**: Proactive planning, accurate peak demand analysis (using smart metering or electrician monitoring), and right-sizing can often avoid immediate electrical panel upgrades for heat pump retrofits. Future electrification plans might necessitate larger upgrades.

### 7. Efficient Heating Upgrade: From Electric Resistance to Heat Pumps in Manufactured Homes

*   **Property Type**: Manufactured homes in Campbell River, B.C.
*   **Initial State**: Electric baseboard or space heaters (high heating costs: **$300-$400/month**), high air leakage, poor insulation.
*   **Key Challenge**: Traditional envelope upgrades are often not cost-effective or practical for manufactured homes; grid constraints from high electric resistance heating demand.
*   **Solution**: Heat pump retrofit program, including free heat pump installation through BC Hydro.
*   **Outcomes**: Improved safety, comfort, and energy performance. Utility bills dropped by **30-40%**. Reduced strain on the local utility grid, freeing up capacity for other transitions.
*   **Key Learning**: Heat pumps offer significant benefits for manufactured homes, reducing utility bills and grid demand, even when extensive envelope upgrades are not feasible.

### 8. Ductless Heat Pump Installation: Best Practices for Manufactured Homes

*   **Property Type**: Manufactured homes.
*   **Key Challenges**: Historically poor insulation and air leakage; add-ons make rule-of-thumb calculations inaccurate; risk of oversizing leading to short cycling; airflow and noise considerations.
*   **Best Practices for Installation**:
    *   **Accurate Heat Loss Calculations**: Essential to avoid oversizing.
    *   **System Selection**: Choose systems with good cold weather performance and sufficient supplementary heat.
    *   **Strategic Placement**: Ductless wall units placed in common areas for optimal airflow.
    *   **Electrical Assessment**: Ensure panels and service can handle new loads.
    *   **Homeowner Education**: Instruct on proper thermostat operation (steady settings, avoid frequent on/off) to maximize heat pump use and minimize backup heat.
*   **Outcomes**: Improved comfort, reduced utility bills (**$85-$120/month savings**), and less strain on the power grid.
*   **Key Learning**: Quality installation, precise calculations, and proper homeowner education are critical for achieving optimal comfort, efficiency, and savings with ductless heat pumps in manufactured homes.

### 9. The Importance of Proactive Planning in Heat Pump Retrofits

*   **Property Type**: 14-year-old suburban home (2200 sq ft).
*   **Initial State**: High-efficiency natural gas forced air furnace with central air conditioning.
*   **Objective**: Electrify heating load, significantly reduce GHG footprint.
*   **Process**: Proactive planning to avoid reactive decisions during equipment failure.
    *   **Energy Audit**: Required for incentives, provided baseline energy score and recommended measures.
    *   **Energy Consultant**: Engaged for performance modeling, backup heating options, and equipment sizing validation.
    *   **Incentives**: Leveraged NRCan's incentive program and 10-year interest-free loan.
    *   **System Choice**: Selected a cold climate air source heat pump with staged electric backup.
*   **Recommendations**: Plan well in advance, consider system configuration, GHG/energy impacts, operating costs, and system size. Invest in right-sizing and choose a qualified contractor.
*   **Key Learning**: Proactive planning, detailed energy assessments, and expert consultation are essential for a successful and financially viable heat pump retrofit.

### 10. Making a Business Case for Gas Furnace Retrofits

*   **Property Type**: Home in Ottawa.
*   **Initial State**: Gas furnace.
*   **Objective**: Transition from gas furnace to heat pump to manage rising utility costs, improve comfort, and achieve greener heating.
*   **Key Challenges**: Capital cost of heat pumps; older homes may have issues with ceiling height, insulation, and increased heating loads.
*   **Energy Efficiency Measures Implemented**:
    *   **Energy Audit**: Homeowner completed an audit and implemented improvements to insulation, windows, and weather stripping, significantly reducing heating requirements.
    *   **Energy Modeling**: Used Hot 2000 to create a model.
    *   **NRCan ASHP Tool**: Used to narrow down heat pump selection.
    *   **Right-Sizing**: A 2.5-ton unit was specified, replacing a 6-ton (72,000 BTU/hr) furnace.
*   **Costs & Savings**:
    *   Capital Cost: **$17,500**.
    *   Rebate: **$5,000**.
    *   Avoided Costs (furnace/AC replacement): **$7,500**.
    *   Net Incremental Cost: **$5,000**.
    *   Utility Bills: **$50 lower on average annually**.
*   **Outcomes**: Significant gas savings, improved comfort, and a hedge against future gas price increases.
*   **Key Learning**: Combining envelope upgrades with data-driven heat pump sizing and leveraging available incentives creates a strong business case for switching from gas furnaces.

### 11. An Introduction to Cold Climate Heat Pumps

*   **Property Type**: 1932 house (1800 sq ft).
*   **Initial State**: 17-year-old 17,000 BTU furnace, old 1.5-ton AC, 100 amp electrical panel.
*   **Objective**: Decarbonize, reduce utility bills, and proactively replace aging equipment.
*   **Energy Efficiency Measures Implemented**:
    *   **Home Energy Audit**: Recommended and led to replacement of insulation and upgrades to doors/windows.
    *   **Envelope Upgrades**: Reduced energy load, allowing for a smaller heat pump system.
    *   **System Installation**: 2.5-ton centrally ducted system using existing ductwork.
    *   **Backup Heat**: 15 kW electric duct heater for temperatures below -30C.
    *   **Electrical Upgrade**: Panel upgraded from 100 to 200 amps to meet energy demands.
*   **Outcomes**: Reliable heating even at -28C/-29C, even temperature distribution, quiet operation. Hydro bills decreased in summer, and **1.5 tonnes of CO2 emissions reduced annually**.
*   **Key Learning**: Cold climate heat pumps are effective in harsh winters. Envelope upgrades reduce system size requirements. Electrical panel upgrades may be necessary for full electrification, but the benefits include substantial GHG reduction and lower operating costs.

### 12. Oil Furnace to Heat Pump Retrofits: Utilizing Existing Distribution Systems

*   **Property Type**: Home in Atlantic Canada with an oil-fired heating system.
*   **Objective**: Transition from oil to heat pumps for reliability, cleanliness, and efficiency.
*   **Key Challenge**: Uncertainty regarding the suitability of existing ductwork for heat pumps.
*   **Energy Efficiency Measures Implemented**:
    *   **Airflow Measurements**: Crucial for determining if existing ductwork could handle the higher airflow requirements of heat pump systems.
    *   **Assessment**: Determined existing ductwork could handle 1200 CFM, sufficient for a 3-ton heat pump (the right size for the house).
*   **Outcomes**: Avoided costly and unnecessary ductwork replacement, ensuring proper sizing and optimal comfort.
*   **Key Learning**: Accurate airflow measurements differentiate contractors and enable them to use existing distribution systems effectively, saving homeowners time and money.

### 13. Oil Furnace to Heat Pump Retrofits: A Whole Home Approach

*   **Property Type**: Homeowner in Halifax, Nova Scotia (17 years old, oil heat).
*   **Initial State**: Oil furnace with a capacity of 72,000 BTU/hr, while the actual heat loss of the home was 26,000 BTU/hr (grossly oversized).
*   **Objective**: Replace oil furnace with a centrally ducted heat pump, improve comfort, save money, and reduce GHG emissions.
*   **Process**: Comprehensive energy assessment and collaboration.
    *   **Certified Energy Evaluator**: Conducted a home energy evaluation to determine insulation levels, air leakage, and actual heat loss.
    *   **Collaboration**: Energy evaluator worked with the contractor and homeowner to develop a comprehensive solution.
*   **Outcomes**: System was properly sized, avoiding oversizing by 50-80% and preventing unnecessary electrical backup. Ensured proper heating and cooling for the home.
*   **Key Learning**: Investing in a comprehensive energy assessment and fostering collaboration between all parties leads to correctly sized heat pump systems, preventing inefficiencies and maximizing long-term benefits.

### 14. A Business Case for Community Housing Retrofits

*   **Property Type**: Ottawa Community Housing (1200 sq ft end-unit townhouse, built 1972).
*   **Initial State**: 15 kW electric furnace, 4.5 kW electric hot water tank, two 5,000 BTU AC units.
*   **Objective**: Achieve housing affordability, climate resilience, and GHG reductions (targeting net-zero by 2050).
*   **Process**: Prioritized occupant comfort and long-term utility costs.
    *   **Pre-work**: Insulation, windows, and weather stripping were upgraded in the preceding five years.
    *   **EnerGuide Evaluation**: Used to determine air tightness, insulation levels, equipment ratings, and cost estimates.
    *   **Detailed Assessment**: Included electrical requirements, space for units, serviceability, and fire/safety considerations.
    *   **Tenant Engagement**: Worked with tenants to understand new systems and maintain comfort.
*   **Outcomes**: **6,400 kWh reduction in electricity (32% of home's consumption)**, and a **180 kg/year reduction in GHG emissions**.
*   **Key Learning**: A holistic approach combining envelope upgrades, detailed assessments, and tenant education is crucial for successful heat pump retrofits in community housing, delivering significant energy and environmental benefits.
