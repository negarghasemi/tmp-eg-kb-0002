# Resiliency Measures to Protect Your Home

**Grant Status:** The **Canada Greener Homes Grant** is now **closed**. December 31, 2025, was the last day to submit documents. Requests submitted by the deadline will continue to be processed.

Climate change can significantly impact your home through environmental factors like fire, flooding, wind damage, and loss of power. This page provides information on additional measures homeowners can take to protect their homes and families from these environmental damages.

*Note: A number of measures were eligible for a grant if they were done in combination with energy-efficiency retrofits. For more information, visit the [Eligible retrofit and grant amounts page](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/home-energy-efficiency/canada-greener-homes-initiative/eligible-retrofits-grant-amounts).*

## Resiliency Measures

### Fire Protection

To make your home more resilient against potential fires, consider:

*   **Exterior Cladding:** Installing non-combustible or fire-resistant exterior cladding materials (e.g., stucco, concrete and fibre cement board, metal siding and brick) when insulating 80% to 100% of exterior walls.
*   **Vents and Gutters:** Installing non-combustible metal mesh covers and screens (3 mm) in gutters, eaves, and vents to reduce flammable vegetation accumulation and limit exposure to sparks and embers.
*   **Roofing Materials:** Replacing existing roofing and eave projections with fire-retardant roofing materials, such as metal, asphalt, clay, and composite rubber tiles with Class A UL/ASTM rating.
*   **Soffits and Eave Projections:** Replacing existing soffits, eave projections, and other roof projections with fire-retardant materials, such as metal, concrete, and fibre cement board.
*   **Service Openings and Vents:** Replacing service openings and vents in exterior walls with fire-resistant materials.

### Flooding Protection

To make your home more resilient against potential flooding or water infiltration, consider:

*   **Sump Pump System:** Installing an alarm with an additional emergency battery backup to a sump pump system.
*   **Backwater Valve:** Installing a backwater valve and alarm to prevent sewage backup during flooding.
*   **Flood Alarms:** Installing flood alarms connected to shutoff the main house water supply valve.
*   **Water Leak Detection:** Installing flood alarms (water leak detection system).
*   **Landscape Water Management:** Practicing landscape water management (rainwater and flood control), including covering over window wells and backfilling grading away from foundations.
*   **Downspouts:** Adding extended downspouts to 1.8 m.
*   **Exterior Walls:** Installing rain screen and strapping on exterior walls (for nearly everything other than vinyl).

**Additional Resources for Flooding Protection:**

### Wind Damage Protection

To make your home more resilient against wind damage, consider:

*   **Shutters and Windows:** Adding hurricane-resistant shutters (roller shutters roll up and down) and windows (aka impact windows or coastal windows).
*   **Window Films:** Installing window blinds and films (security or solar).
*   **Roof Trusses:** Installing hurricane straps or special self-tapping screws (making sure roof trusses are securely fastened to the wall below).
*   **Roof Sheathing:** Adding fasteners (screws) to roof sheathing, wall sealant, and flashing under roof shingles.
*   **Foundation Anchors:** Adding hurricane anchors between walls and foundation.
*   **Roof and Wall Fastening:** Incorporating roof and wall fastening (re-nailing).
*   **Sealant and Flashing:** Installing sealant and flashing under roof shingles.

### Power Failure Resiliency

To make your home more resilient to power failures, consider:

*   **Battery Backup:** Adding a standby emergency battery backup-standalone system.
*   **Generators:** Adding standby non-portable emergency power sources (e.g., generators).

## Canada Greener Homes Initiative

### Programs

*   **Oil to Heat Pump Affordability program:** Helps low- to median-income homeowners switch from oil heating to an eligible electric heat pump system. Details vary by province and territory.
*   **Canada Greener Homes Affordability Program:** Helps low- to median-income homeowners and tenants reduce their energy bills and greenhouse gas emissions through no-cost home retrofits, delivered through provincial and territorial partners, with additional support for Indigenous organizations.
