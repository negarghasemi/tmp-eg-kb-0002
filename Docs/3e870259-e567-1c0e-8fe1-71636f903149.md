# Introduction to the Regulations

_This document provides an overview of Canada's Energy Efficiency Regulations, administered by Natural Resources Canada (NRCan). It outlines the requirements for dealers importing or shipping energy-using products for sale or lease across Canada._

### Video Summary: How to meet the requirements of the Energy Efficiency Regulations

This video explains that all regulated products must meet federal energy efficiency standards and follow the Energy Efficiency Regulations to be imported into Canada or shipped between provinces or territories for sale or lease. Dealers, such as manufacturers, importers, sellers, or leasers of regulated products, must ensure: 

*   Each product model is **energy efficiency verified** by a Standards Council of Canada accredited body.
*   A **verification mark** is present on the product before it leaves their possession.
*   An **energy efficiency report** is submitted to NRCan *before* importation or inter-provincial shipment.
*   **Customs release documents** for imports include required product details, the dealer's address, and the purpose of import.
*   Products are **properly labelled** (e.g., EnerGuide for appliances) if applicable.

Failure to comply, such as incomplete customs documents or not meeting standards, can lead to products being refused customs clearance.

## Canadas Energy Efficiency Act and Energy Efficiency Regulations

_In the Regulations, **province** means a province of Canada and includes Yukon, the Northwest Territories and Nunavut._

The *Energy Efficiency Act* and *Energy Efficiency Regulations* are administered by Natural Resources Canada (NRCan). This introduction provides a general overview of the requirements.

**[Find more details on the Act and Regulations](https://laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2016-311/index.html)**

### Purpose of the Regulations

The *Energy Efficiency Regulations* apply to **dealers** who:

*   Import regulated energy-using products into Canada for sale or lease, OR
*   Ship regulated energy-using products manufactured in one Canadian province or territory to another for sale or lease.

**A dealer** is defined as a person engaged in the business of:

1.  Manufacturing energy-using products in Canada.
2.  Importing energy-using products into Canada.
3.  Selling or leasing energy-using products obtained directly or indirectly from a person engaged in a business described in (1) or (2) or an agent thereof.

### Regulated Products

The *Energy Efficiency Regulations* apply to energy-using products in the following categories:

*   **Household appliances**
*   **Water heaters**
*   **Heating and air-conditioning equipment**
*   **Lighting products**
*   **Electronic products**
*   **Refrigeration equipment**
*   **Other commercial and industrial products**

All these products must meet federal energy efficiency standards to be imported into Canada or shipped between provinces/territories for sale or lease. Unless specified otherwise, these regulations still apply even if the product is incorporated into a larger, unregulated unit.

**[Search the product list](https://spl-lpi.nrcan-rncan.gc.ca/en-US/)**

### Responsibilities of Dealers

Dealers are responsible for ensuring that each model of a regulated product they import or ship meets the energy efficiency standard. They must also ensure:

*   The product bears an **energy efficiency verification mark**.
*   An **EnerGuide label, lighting product label, or walk-in refrigeration nameplate** is affixed to each unit, if required.
*   An **energy efficiency report** has been submitted to NRCan for the specific product model before importation or inter-provincial shipment.
*   **Additional import information** is included in customs release documents at the time of importation.

#### In summary, the dealer must:

-   Ensure the product meets the **energy efficiency standard** specified in the Regulations.
-   Ensure an **energy efficiency report** has been filed with NRCan.
-   Provide the necessary **import information** to Canada Border Services Agency, when importing products.
-   Ensure the product bears an **energy efficiency verification mark**.
-   Ensure an **EnerGuide label, lighting product label or nameplate** is affixed to the product (if prescribed by the Regulations).

### Energy Efficiency Standards

The prescribed energy efficiency standard for each regulated product is detailed in the product-specific sections of the Regulations.

_The Regulations may incorporate standards from other jurisdictions. If such a standard is repealed, the reference continues to apply as it read on the day before repeal._

For each regulated product, NRCan provides individual product pages that include:

*   **Definition** of the product.
*   **Standard** for testing energy efficiency.
*   **Compliance date** for meeting the standard.
*   **Product-specific information** required for the energy efficiency report and template links.
*   Information on **labeling requirements** (EnerGuide or lighting product label/nameplate).
*   Availability of a **searchable product list** of compliant models.

### Energy Efficiency Report

_Energy performance information provided in the energy efficiency report may be generated by a mathematical model that emulates the manner in which the information is collected under the identified testing standard._

An energy efficiency report must be filed with NRCan **before** shipping an energy-using product between provinces or territories, or importing it, *unless* a report has already been submitted for that model.

#### Required Data in the Energy Efficiency Report:

-   **Name of product**
-   **Brand name**
-   **Model number**
-   **Manufacturer**
-   **Name of the certification body** or province/territory that verified the product's energy performance and authorized the verification mark.
-   Indication of whether a **mathematical model** was used to generate any product-specific information.
-   **Specific information** about the energy efficiency and energy-use characteristics of the product, as listed on individual product pages.

#### Filing the Energy Efficiency Report

Dealers only need to submit a report if the product model is not already listed in NRCan's database. Products on NRCan's searchable product list can be imported/shipped, provided no changes affecting energy efficiency have been made.

#### Submitting the Report to Natural Resources Canada

Reports can be submitted electronically using the **Compliance Regulatory Energy Efficiency Database (CREED)**. Certification bodies can also be authorized to submit documentation on behalf of dealers or manufacturers.

**[Energy efficiency report template](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/energy-efficiency-regulations/energy-efficiency-reporting-eer-templates)**

#### NRCan Reviews the Report

NRCan verifies if the product meets the energy efficiency standard. Compliant models are added to NRCan's searchable product list. If a product does not meet the standard, NRCan will contact the dealer to resolve the issue before importation or shipment.

NRCan cross-matches import information from the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA) with its database to ensure compliance. NRCan can instruct customs officials to stop the importation of non-compliant products.

### Import Reporting

A dealer importing a regulated energy-using product must include specific information on the customs release document at the time of release.

**Exemptions**: The following products are exempt from providing this information *only if incorporated into another product*:

*   Battery chargers
*   External power supplies
*   Fluorescent lamp ballasts
*   Electric motors
*   Small electric motors

#### Information Required on the Customs Release Document:

-   **Name of product** (refer to the list of energy-using products)
-   **Model number**
-   **Brand name** (if any)
-   **Address of the dealer** who is importing the product
-   **Purpose for which the product is being imported**:
    *   For sale or lease in Canada without modification.
    *   For sale or lease in Canada after being modified to comply with the prescribed energy efficiency standard.
    *   For use as a component in a product being exported from Canada.

_If the customs release document is incomplete or if the product does not meet energy efficiency standards, the customs officer may refuse to allow the product to clear customs._

The customs release document must be submitted electronically to CBSA. Required information must always be included, regardless of when the product was manufactured.

### Energy Efficiency Verification Mark

Regulated energy-using products must bear an energy efficiency verification mark from a certification body accredited by the Standards Council of Canada. **Exemptions** apply to CFLs and an alternative exists for external power supplies (as per section 4 of the Regulations).

#### Verification Mark

An energy efficiency verification mark indicates that the product's energy performance has been verified. _It is not a safety certification mark._ It signifies compliance with Canada's energy efficiency standard (if applicable) and verification of energy performance information.

The certification body must be accredited by the Standards Council of Canada for energy efficiency certification. Provincial or territorial labels may also be accepted if they meet or exceed federal standards.

#### Verification Mark Placement

The verification mark must be readily visible on the product's surface. For battery chargers, external power supplies, and certain lamps (CFL, general service lamps, modified spectrum incandescent lamps, general service fluorescent lamps, general service incandescent reflector lamps), it can be affixed to the product's exterior packaging.

The mark must be on the product before it leaves the dealer's possession (or the consignee's possession).

### Labelling Requirements

The Act and Regulations require dealers to attach a bilingual (English and French) label to the following energy-using products:

*   Clothes dryers
*   Clothes washers
*   Integrated clothes washer-dryers
*   Dishwashers
*   Electric ranges
*   Freezers
*   Refrigerators and combination refrigerator-freezers
*   Miscellaneous refrigeration products
*   Room air conditioners

#### Lighting Product Label

Required on the packaging for general service lamps (GSL) manufactured on or after January 1st, 2027. GSLs include:

*   Compact fluorescent lamps
*   General service incandescent lamps
*   General service incandescent reflector lamps
*   General service light-emitting diode (LED) lamps
*   General service organic light-emitting diode (OLED) lamps

NRCan provides [label design templates](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/energy-efficiency-regulations/general-service-lamp-labelling-design-templates?_gl=1*1uht8pa*_ga*MTcwNzA1MDUzNy4xNzU0NTEzNDY4*_ga_C2N57Y7DX5*czE3Njk4MDE5NTckbzI4MyRnMSR0MTc2OTgwMjE0NCRqNjAkbDAkaDA.) to meet GSL labelling requirements.

#### Walk-in Refrigeration Nameplate

Required on the outside of the product, readily visible prior to assembly, for:

*   **Components of walk-in coolers and walk-in freezers** (door assembly, panel, dedicated condensing refrigeration system, and matched refrigeration systems), with varying manufacturing dates for compliance.

#### Labelling Protocol

Labels must be affixed before the product leaves the dealer's ( or consignee's) possession and remain until the product is sold or leased at retail.

### Details on EnerGuide, Lighting Product, and Walk-in Refrigeration Labels

*   **EnerGuide label**: Indicates estimated annual energy consumption in kWh for household appliances. Room air conditioners show the CEER (combined energy efficiency ratio).
*   **Lighting product label**: Displays light output (lumens), energy used (watts), efficacy (lumens/watt), bulb life (hours), and light appearance (Kelvin).
*   **Walk-in refrigeration nameplate**: Identifies brand/manufacturer, intended use (cooler, freezer, or both), model number, date of manufacture, and if for indoor use only. This prevents installation errors.

_For more labelling details:_

*   [EnerGuide labelling instructions, and labelling scales for major household appliances and room air conditioners](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/product-energy-ratings/energuide/energuide-labelling-instructions-labelling-scales-appliances-room-air-conditioners)
*   [Lighting product lamp label as per sections 425 to 429 of the Regulations](http://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2016-311/)
*   [Walk-in refrigeration component nameplate as per section 658 of the Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2016-311/)

### Exemptions

Dealers may be exempt from submitting an energy efficiency report and products from meeting the prescribed standard under certain circumstances:

1.  **Modification for compliance**: If a product is imported/shipped to be modified to meet the standard. The dealer has 90 days for modification and 120 days to submit an energy efficiency report to NRCan.
2.  **Component for export**: If a product is imported/shipped to be incorporated into another product and then exported from Canada.
3.  **Direct export**: If a product is imported/shipped solely for the purpose of being exported from Canada.

### Federal and Provincial Energy Efficiency Legislation

Governments of British Columbia, Manitoba, Ontario, Quebec, New Brunswick, and Nova Scotia also regulate energy efficiency. Provincial/territorial regulations may differ from federal requirements or apply to other equipment. For federally regulated products, see also the [energy efficiency regulations by province](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/energy-efficiency-regulations/energy-products-regulated-federally-provincially).
