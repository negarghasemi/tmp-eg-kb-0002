# All About Gas Fireplaces

*Produced by Natural Resources Canada's Office of Energy Efficiency - EnerGuide*

EnerGuide provides home energy evaluations and ratings to help homeowners plan for energy efficiency renovations and make informed decisions.

## Chapter 1: A Special Place in the Home

Modern homes are often incompatible with conventional wood-burning fireplaces due to increased insulation and airtightness, which cause inefficiency and poor air quality. Gas fireplaces offer a popular alternative, providing convenience, cleanliness, and safety.

**Advantages of Gas Fireplaces:**
- **Convenience:** On/off switch, ever-present fuel supply.
- **Cleanliness:** No ashes, wood chips, or bark.
- **Maintenance:** Elimination of chimney cleaning.
- **Safety:** Sealed combustion units reduce toxic combustion gas spillage.
- **Environmental Benefits:** Compared to conventional wood fireplaces.

*Not all gas fireplaces are created equal; some are highly efficient, others are not. Vent-free technology can cause indoor air quality problems.*

*This information applies to both natural gas and propane fireplaces.*

## Chapter 2: Gas Fireplaces and the Environment

Gas fireplaces are known for clean-burning characteristics, producing less carbon monoxide and particulate emissions than wood fireplaces.

**Environmental Considerations:**
- Natural gas and propane release nitrogen oxides (contributing to smog) and carbon dioxide (a greenhouse gas).
- They also release significant amounts of moisture.
- A poorly adjusted gas fireplace can generate incomplete combustion products, including carbon monoxide.
- Buying an energy-efficient gas fireplace and using it wisely reduces energy consumption and greenhouse gas emissions.

## Chapter 3: What to Look For

### Types of Gas Fireplaces
-   **Inserts:** Convert existing wood-burning masonry or factory-built metal fireplaces to gas. Require relining existing chimneys with an approved vent.
-   **Zero-clearance gas fireplaces:** Used in new constructions or renovations where no existing fireplace is present. Can be installed within the house envelope, even against an outside wall. Requires careful adherence to manufacturer's clearances and approved venting.
-   **Free-standing gas fireplaces:** Resemble wood-burning stoves, often more effective at heating as all surfaces are exposed to the room.

*Glass fronts can be tempered or ceramic; ceramic glass is better at transmitting infrared heat and withstands higher temperatures.*

### Types of Venting
-   **Natural draft venting:** Uses a vertical chimney (B-vent or approved metal liner) and natural draft. Requires extra house air via a draft hood.
-   **Power venting:** Uses an electrical fan to assist venting, allowing horizontal and vertical flues in various locations. Improves efficiency and uses less house air.
-   **Direct-vent fireplace:** Draws outdoor combustion air through one pipe and exhausts products through another. Units are sealed, requiring no room air for combustion, preventing heat loss. Typically installed on an outside wall.

    -   **Coaxial venting:** Two concentric pipes (outer for intake, inner for exhaust). Common for sidewall venting.
    -   **Collinear venting:** Two separate pipes (one for intake, one for exhaust). Used when space is limited or for existing fireplace retrofits, or where condensation/icing are concerns.

*For energy-efficient R-2000 homes, gas fireplaces must be direct-vented (sealed) or power-vented for optimal protection against combustion product spillage and increased efficiency.*

### Efficiency Ratings
-   **Steady-state measurements:** Historically used, representing maximum efficiency under laboratory conditions. Do not reflect real-world heat loss, leading to misleading results.
-   **EnerGuide Fireplace Efficiency (FE) rating:** Based on the CSA-P.4 test method (CSA P.4.1-02 standard). This standardized method measures annual fireplace efficiency, considering overall operation throughout the heating season.
    -   FE ratings range from **30% to 70%** for better units.
    -   Expressed as a percentage; **higher is more efficient**.
    -   Appears on the EnerGuide label, indicating standardized testing.

#### EnerGuide Label for Gas Fireplaces
-   Labels show the FE rating (e.g., 72% for a single model) or list it next to each model number if multiple models are shown.
-   Both labels state \"Based on CSA P.4.1-02\" to confirm testing method.

### Energy Efficiency Considerations
-   **Application:** Determine if the unit is for decoration or heating.
-   **Sizing:** Ensure the unit is appropriately sized for the space to avoid overheating.
-   **Zone Heating vs. Central Heating:** Understand the difference.
-   **EnerGuide Rating:** Every model has one; higher numbers indicate better efficiency.
-   **Cost Savings:** Actual savings depend on location, climate, fuel cost, and house efficiency.
-   **Comparison:** Be careful when comparing ratings for \"decorative\" vs. \"heater\" appliances.

### Input and Output Ratings
-   **Input rating:** Fuel energy consumed per hour (Btu/h or GJ/h).
-   **Output rating:** Heat supplied to the house (Btu/h or GJ/h).
-   **Key Point:** High input does not guarantee high output. Output depends on input *and* efficiency.
    -   Example: A 20,000 Btu/h fireplace at 70% efficiency provides the same heat as a 40,000 Btu/h unit at 35% efficiency, using half the fuel.
-   **Sizing:** Choose based on heat output, not input. Oversizing can lead to overheating and wasted fuel.
-   **Control:** Look for features like modulation (turndown) or heat distribution (ducting) to manage heat output.

### Electronic or Intermittent Ignition vs. Continuous Pilot Lights
-   **Pilot light:** A small continuous flame (600 to 1500 Btu/h) that ignites the main burner. Leaving it on continuously increases energy costs.
    -   **Recommendation:** Turn off the pilot light when the fireplace is not in frequent use.
-   **Automatic starter (Electronic or intermittent ignition):** Eliminates the need for a continuous pilot light, saving energy.
    -   **Preference:** If a continuous pilot is preferred for auxiliary heat during power outages, choose a unit with an easy on/off and relighting mechanism.

### Variable-Setting Controls and Thermostats
-   **Variable-setting control (Turndown):** Adjusts heat output by regulating gas consumption. Allows for better comfort, prevents overheating, and maintains flame visibility.
    -   **Recommendation:** Look for a wide turndown range (e.g., down to 20% of full load).
-   **Ductwork:** Some fireplaces can connect to ductwork to distribute heat to other areas of the home, preventing localized overheating.
-   **Automatic thermostat controls:** Adjust firing rate to maintain room temperature. Effective with wide turndown ranges; less so with limited turndown (can cause frequent on/off cycling and energy waste).

### Heat Exchange and Transfer
-   Gas fireplaces transfer heat via **radiation** and **convection**.
-   **Radiation:** Transfer of heat from flames and hot surfaces to objects (furniture, walls, people) via infrared radiation. Visible flame provides comfortable radiant heat.
-   **Convection:** Circulation of room air, picking up heat from fireplace surfaces. Cool air enters, heats up, rises, and circulates throughout the room.
    -   **Surface Area:** Free-standing and hearth-mounted units with more exposed surface area tend to have higher efficiencies due to better radiation and convection.
-   **Heat exchanger:** Extracts more heat from combustion gases and transfers it to the house (secondary heat exchange).
-   **Variable-speed fireplace fans:** Increase convective heat transfer and air circulation. Look for quiet fans.
-   **Ceiling fan:** Can improve circulation of heated air from a fireplace.

## Chapter 4: Locating a Fireplace for Maximum Benefit

Proper placement of an efficient gas fireplace is crucial to its contribution to home heating.

**Location Recommendations:**
-   Place in a major living area (e.g., family room, dining room, living room, kitchen) where it is visually attractive and where occupants spend most time.
-   **Open floor plans** and accessible stairwells allow heat to move to other rooms and upstairs.
-   Can effectively lower heating costs in homes with electric baseboards by providing primary comfort heat in main areas.
-   If installing against an outside wall, build the fireplace *inside* the house envelope or use a unit with an **insulated outer casing**.
-   Locate the fireplace and chimney on an **inside wall** to ensure better draft and reduce chances of the house acting as a chimney (negative pressure drawing combustion gases back into the home).
-   In basements, a **direct-vent unit** is best as it doesn't require a chimney and prevents combustion exhaust spillage. Insulate the basement before installation. Consider low-input units or those with ducts for heat transfer.

### The house as a chimney
-   An operating chimney creates natural vertical draft as warm air rises.
-   In winter, a house also creates its own draft (stack effect), with higher pressure at the top and lower pressure at the bottom.
-   If an unsealed (non-direct-vent) gas fireplace is in a home with lower pressure than outside, the house can become a more effective chimney than the fireplace chimney itself (especially if the chimney is on an outside wall).
-   This can cause **reverse flow of air**, leading to spillage of combustion gases (including carbon monoxide) into the home, creating hazardous indoor air quality problems.

## Chapter 5: Summary of Features to Look for in an Energy-Efficient Gas Fireplace

When selecting a gas fireplace, prioritize models with high EnerGuide FE ratings and suitable heat output for your space.

**Key Features for Energy Efficiency:**
-   **High EnerGuide Fireplace Efficiency (FE) rating** (tested to CSA P.4.1-02, found in product brochures or websites).
-   **Direct-vent design** (where appropriate).
-   **Intermittent electronic ignition system** or an easy way to turn off/relight the pilot light.
-   **Ceramic glass front**.
-   **Quiet squirrel-cage-type circulating fan** for convective heat transfer.
-   **Secondary heat exchanger**.
-   **Insulated outer casing** (if located on an exterior wall) to prevent heat loss.
-   **Good turndown** or other means (e.g., ducting) to prevent localized overheating.

## Chapter 6: Gas Logs Not Recommended

-   Ceramic \"logs\" with gas burners placed into existing wood fireplaces are **not recommended**.
-   They provide no real heat, waste energy and money, and are a potential source of pollutants.
-   Can cause venting problems in colder regions due to low-temperature, low-flow, high-moisture flue gases not suited for wood fireplace chimneys.
-   Risk of flue gas condensation and chimney deterioration if not relined.
-   If on an outside wall, burners may not generate enough heat for a good draft, leading to combustion gases being drawn back into the living area.

## Chapter 7: What to Avoid

-   **Vent-free gas fireplaces:** These units do not vent to the outdoors; all combustion gases (carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, nitrogen oxides, water vapour) are released directly into the house.
-   They cause serious indoor air-quality and moisture problems, especially in airtight Canadian homes.
-   **Not approved for use in Canada.**

## Chapter 8: Installing Your Gas Fireplace

-   Ensure installation is performed by a **qualified technician**.
-   Check local requirements for **permits** and **inspections**.
-   Inform your **insurance company**.
-   Obtain **several estimates** from contractors, considering their knowledge, experience, and materials.
-   Estimates should include:
    -   Itemized list of labor and material costs, total cost.
    -   Clear definition of responsibility for permits, fees, and on-site inspections.
    -   Scheduling of utility work (e.g., supply pipe installation).
    -   Clear completion timeline.
    -   Warranty for materials and labor.
    -   Payment schedule and method.
-   Check **references** and with the **Better Business Bureau**.
-   Ask for clear explanations from the installer.

## Chapter 9: Safety Tips for Operating Your Gas Fireplace

-   Do not operate during construction/renovation until the area is thoroughly cleaned (drywall dust can harm components).
-   Check manufacturer's instructions for mantle/surround modifications (minimum clearances).
-   Have the fireplace **inspected before initial use and regularly serviced**.
-   Read manufacturer's instructions carefully before first operation.
-   Keep **combustibles away** from hot surfaces.
-   If pilot/flame goes out, wait 5+ minutes (per instructions) before relighting to clear gas.
-   Be alert for **unusual odours or flames**; contact dealer for servicing. Contact gas company if you smell gas.
-   Keep air-circulating passageways and fans clean and unobstructed.
-   Wait for the unit to cool completely before wiping glass (to prevent thermal stress/breakage).
-   If glass breaks, have a qualified technician replace it with manufacturer-supplied glass.
-   For sidewall-exhausting, direct-vent units, keep the external vent clear of debris/snow and maintain minimum clearances from fences, shrubs, etc.
-   Consider installing a **carbon monoxide detector** near unsealed (non-direct-vent) fireplaces. If it alarms, open windows, shut off fireplace, leave the house, and seek medical attention if symptoms occur. Have a professional remedy the source of CO.

## Chapter 10: Using Your Gas Fireplace Efficiently

Maximize performance and minimize energy consumption by following these guidelines:

-   **Pilot light:** If your unit has one, turn it off during summer and heating season when not frequently used. Follow relighting instructions carefully.
-   **Thermostat:** Turn down your home's main thermostat when the fireplace is operating. If the fireplace has its own thermostat, keep it at the lowest acceptable comfort setting and turn it down when the room is unused.
-   **Glass:** Keep the glass clean to maximize radiant heat transfer.

### Step 3: Determine your home's annual heating load

**Formula:**
`Annual Heating Load (GJ) = (Heating Bill / 100,000) x (Seasonal Efficiency / Energy Cost/Unit) x Energy Content`

*Example Calculation:* If annual gas heating bill is $687, gas costs $0.22/m, and furnace efficiency is 60%, with energy content of 37.5 MJ/m:
`Annual Heating Load = (687 / 100,000) x (60 / 0.22) x 37.5 = 70 GJ`
