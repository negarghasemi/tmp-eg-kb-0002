# Heat pump water heaters

*May 2024*

## Technical bulletin on developing the standards

**Webinar**: Wednesday, June 5, 1:00-2:30 pm EDT

In April 2024, the Office of Energy Efficiency at Natural Resources Canada (NRCan) updated its [Forward Regulatory Plan](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/node/21709#msdynttrid=gPrvBKD4QnHLNydHNDucp-nQokQOZTaBRjIp6V7eiWk), identifying its intention to amend Canadas *Energy Efficiency Regulations* (the Regulations) to introduce heat pump water heaters as a new energy-using product, including energy efficiency and testing standards, harmonized with the United States.

This technical bulletin initiates the consultation process to collect stakeholder views on the proposed requirements. Input will inform the development of future amendments. NRCan will also conduct a cost-benefit analysis using Canadian market data to assess economic and environmental impacts.

The Government of Canada intends to pre-publish a regulatory proposal in the *Canada Gazette*, Part I, in 2025.

## Background

Heat pump water heaters are not currently defined as an energy-using product in the Regulations. Water heaters are significant energy consumers in buildings, and efficiency standards for heat pump water heaters would lead to energy savings and reduced costs for Canadians. These standards encourage the adoption of more efficient product designs.

In 2021, NRCan and the U.S. Department of Energy (U.S. DOE) signed a *Memorandum of Understanding* to collaborate on energy efficiency and testing standards. On May 6, 2024, the U.S. DOE published a Final Rule for Consumer Water Heaters, including updated energy efficiency standards for household heat pump hot water heaters.

NRCan is considering amending the Regulations to harmonize household heat pump water heater requirements with U.S. DOE energy efficiency and testing standards. NRCan is also considering harmonizing testing standards for commercial heat pump water heaters, but no energy efficiency standards are currently under consideration for them.

## Product description

A **heat pump water heater** is being considered to be defined as a heat pump-type unit utilizing electricity to heat potable water upon demand, designed to transfer thermal energy, and including all ancillary equipment. 

A **household heat pump water heater** is being considered to be defined as a heat pump water heater with a maximum current rating  24 amperes at a voltage  250 volts and an effective storage volume  76 L (20 U.S. gal) and  454 L (120 U.S. gal).

A **commercial heat pump water heater** is being considered to be defined as a heat pump water heater with a maximum current rating > 24 amperes or a voltage > 250 volts. This includes air-source, water-source, and direct geo-exchange heat pump water heaters.

A **split-system heat pump water heater** is being considered to be defined as one where at least the compressor (which may be installed outdoors) is separate from the storage tank.

A **unitary heat pump water heater** is being considered to be a factory-packaged heat pump water heater, excluding split-type.

A **small heat pump water heater** is being considered to be a household heat pump water heater with an effective storage volume  76 L (20 U.S. gal) and  133 L (35 U.S. gal) and a first-hour rating < 193 L (51 U.S. gal).

## Testing standard

NRCan is considering referencing the following testing standards, using an ambulatory incorporation by reference (as amended from time to time):

*   **Household heat pump water heaters**: [Appendix E to Subpart B of Part 430, Title 10](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-10/part-430/appendix-Appendix E to Subpart B of Part 430) to the U.S. Code of Federal Regulations, entitled *Uniform Test Method for Measuring the Energy Consumption of Water Heaters*.
*   **Commercial heat pump water heaters**: [Appendix E to Subpart G of Part 431, Title 10](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-10/part-431/appendix-Appendix E to Subpart G of Part 431) to the U.S. Code of Federal Regulations, entitled *Uniform Test Method for the Measurement of Energy Efficiency of Commercial Heat Pump Water Heaters*.

## Important dates

These modifications to the Regulations would come into force **six months** after the date of publication in the *Canada Gazette*, Part II.

NRCan is considering applying the new energy efficiency standards to household heat pump water heaters based on the periods of manufacture indicated in Table 2.

Additionally, NRCan is considering applying reporting and verification requirements to household and commercial heat pump water heaters manufactured on or after **January 1, 2026**.

### Regulatory pathway

NRCan is considering amending the Regulations through Governor in Council amendment. The proposed changes would be pre-published in the *Canada Gazette*, Part I, followed by a public comment period. Final publication in the *Canada Gazette*, Part II, is typically within a year of the initial public comment period.

## Labelling requirements

NRCan is **not** considering labelling requirements for heat pump water heaters.

## Verification requirements

NRCan would require heat pump water heaters to carry a **third-party verification mark** indicating compliance with energy efficiency standards (if applicable) and verification of reported information.

The verification mark must be from a Standards Council of Canada accredited certification body operating an energy efficiency certification program for the product.

## Reporting requirements

### Energy efficiency report

The energy efficiency report for heat pump water heaters would include the following information:

-   **Name of product**: Heat pump water heater
-   **Brand name**
-   **Model number**
-   **Name of the manufacturer**
-   **Name of the certification body** whose verification mark would be affixed to the product
-   **Whether a mathematical model was used** to generate any of the product-specific information
-   **Duty of product**: Household or commercial
-   **Rated storage volume** in litres
-   **Nominal voltage**

For **household heat pump water heaters**, the report would also include:

-   **Type of product**: Unitary or split-type
-   **Effective storage volume** in litres
-   **First-hour rating**
-   **Uniform Energy Factor**
-   **Recovery efficiency**
-   **Nominal power input of upper and lower resistance elements**: W (Report 0 watts if none)

For **commercial heat pump water heaters**, the report would also include:

-   **Coefficient of performance (COP<sub>h</sub>)**

The Regulations apply to products imported or shipped inter-provincially for sale or lease in Canada. An energy efficiency report must be submitted by the dealer to NRCan **before** the product is first imported into Canada or traded inter-provincially.

### Import report

A dealer importing these products into Canada would include the following information on the customs release document:

-   **Name of product**
-   **Model number**
-   **Brand name**
-   **Address of the dealer** importing the product
-   **Purpose for which the product is being imported**: (e.g., for sale or lease in Canada without modification; for sale or lease in Canada after modification to comply with energy efficiency standards; or for use as a component in a product being exported from Canada)
