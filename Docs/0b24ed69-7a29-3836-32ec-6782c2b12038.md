# Step 3. Retrofit your home

## Grant Status
**Closed: Canada Greener Homes Grant.** December 31, 2025, was the last day to submit your documents. Requests submitted by the deadline will continue to be processed.

*The completed pre-retrofit EnerGuide evaluation, along with the EnerGuide label, Homeowner Information Sheet, and Renovation Upgrade Report, helped homeowners decide what recommended retrofits to complete before the deadline.*

## Retrofit Eligibility
*   Only retrofit work done after **December 1, 2020**, that includes both a pre- and post-retrofit EnerGuide evaluation qualified for a grant. Evaluations completed before December 1, 2020, are not eligible.
*   You had to undertake **at least one retrofit that was both eligible and recommended** by your energy advisor in their report to receive the contribution towards the evaluations and a grant.
*   For a retrofit to be eligible for a grant, it must have been **recommended by your energy advisor** and listed in your Renovation Upgrade Report.

### Recommended Retrofits are Prioritized by:
*   Your potential energy savings
*   The life expectancy of your homes components
*   The interactions among the components in your home
*   Your potential renovation plans
*   The cost

## Considerations before starting

### Your home operates as a system
*All of its elements  the walls, roof, ventilation, heating and cooling systems, external environment, and even the activities of the occupants  affect one another. How these elements interact determines your homes overall performance. For example, poor insulation or ventilation can cancel out an investment in new windows or doors.*

### Some retrofits may not be eligible
*The Canada Greener Homes Grant does not provide grants for all the recommended retrofits in the renovation upgrade report. The energy advisor may have recommended some retrofits that were not eligible for a grant but could still help improve the energy efficiency, indoor air quality and comfort of your home.*

### To receive a grant for the eligible retrofits
*   It was highly recommended that you obtain proof of the installers qualifications to install equipment (sometimes issued by your province or territory).
*   If you decided to replace your windows or heating system, you had to choose an eligible product from the list to be eligible for the grant.
*   For solar photovoltaic systems: no national or provincial certifications for designers/installers. For off-grid systems, ensure design and installation comply with local building and electrical codes.

### Your budget plays a major role in deciding which retrofits to do
*   Program participants needed to pay upfront for both EnerGuide evaluations and retrofits.
*   The grant may not fully cover your expenses.
    *   Up to **$600** maximum contribution toward the total cost of your pre- and post-retrofit EnerGuide evaluations (if you completed at least one eligible retrofit and all required steps).
    *   Between **$125 and $5,000** to help with costs for eligible home retrofits.
*   *The loan program is now closed to new applicants.*

### There may be additional incentives available to you
*   Your retrofits may have been eligible for provincial, territorial, municipal, or non-governmental organization incentives.
*   Other incentive programs used by homeowners are considered when determining final grant eligibility and amounts under the Canada Greener Homes Grant initiative.

## Document requirements

### Document your home retrofit and verify expenses
*   Keep all quotes, invoices, receipts, proof of work done, and attestation forms.
*   Invoices and receipts must be marked **paid in full** and show company information, invoice number/date, your name/address, description of work, product eligibility information, cost, and proof of payment.
*   **Homeowners must keep all documents until March 31, 2028.**

### Mandatory documents
*   Receipts for the pre- and post-retrofit EnerGuide evaluation.
*   All receipts and invoices, marked paid in full, for products purchased and for installation costs.

### Other recommended documents (if applicable)
*   For grid-connected solar photovoltaic systems: copy of the letter of approval or permission for interconnection.
*   Attestation form confirming:
    *   Mechanical system installed by a trained and licensed professional.
    *   Ground source heat pump system installed in accordance with CSA standards.
    *   Air source heat pump or cold climate air source heat pump system capable of distributing heat to the entire home.
    *   For off-grid systems, system designed and installed in accordance with local building and electrical codes.

## Planning your retrofits

### Professional Installation
*   **Retrofits that require a licensed and trained professional:**
    *   Heat pumps
    *   Heat pump water heaters
    *   Furnaces (*eligible for northern and off-grid community residents only*)
    *   Boilers (*eligible for northern and off-grid community residents only*)
*   **Retrofits for which professionals are strongly recommended (may be required in some jurisdictions):**
    *   Renewable energy (solar photovoltaic systems)
    *   Air sealing
    *   Home insulation
    *   Windows and doors
    *   Thermostats
    *   Resiliency measures

### Doing it yourself
*   Personal labour costs are **not eligible** for a grant.
*   Always prioritize health and safety.
*   Use tools and products according to manufacturers directions.
*   Wear appropriate protective clothing and equipment.
*   Protect your home and family from dust, debris, and possible contaminants.
*   Take precautions when working with vermin, mould, lead, asbestos, vermiculite insulation, and other hazardous materials.
*   Purchase and install products that are eligible for a grant.

### Benefits of an energy-efficient home
*When you make retrofits to your home, you ensure that it is more comfortable and more affordable, and you contribute to a better planet.*

## Do your homework
*   You are responsible for choosing products and materials and getting necessary building and utility permits.
*   If no permit was required, you and your contractor are responsible for ensuring all products, services, and installations meet relevant building codes and standards.
*   Compare contractors, get written quotes and contracts, and keep all receipts and invoices.

### Hiring a contractor
**Ask any prospective contractor questions such as:**
*   May I contact your references?
*   Can you provide proof of your licence to install equipment?
*   Will this project comply with local building codes, bylaws, and other legislation?
*   Will it require building or utility permits?
*   Do the products you recommend meet all applicable legislation?
*   Can you provide any available Material Safety Data Sheets?
*   What experience do you and your team have?
*   What challenges have you experienced?
*   What steps do you take to protect my home and family?

**Before you begin a retrofit:**
*   Get estimates from local contracting and retrofit service providers and schedule the work.
*   Plan your work and get the correct permits.
*   Contact your home insurance provider regarding your policy.
*   When using a licensed professional, obtain proof of their licence.
*   Make sure you purchase and install products that are eligible for a grant.

### Choosing products
*   All products must be **purchased in Canada** (online purchases eligible only if from a Canadian online distributor).
*   Get facts about products you or your contractor intend to use.
*   For some retrofits, you must purchase and install products that are eligible for the grant.
*   Learn and follow proper installation techniques.
*   Ensure products meet Canadian product standards (compliance mark/stamp or CCMC evaluation number).
*   Find out about health and safety issues; request Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS) if applicable and follow safety guidelines.

### Health and safety considerations
*   **Air sealing:** Can cause indoor air quality issues or excessive condensation if ventilation is not adjusted. Seek advice from an energy advisor and qualified ventilation contractor.
*   **Urea-Formaldehyde-Based Foam Insulation (UFFI):** Prohibited in Canada since 1980 due to formaldehyde gas release.
*   **Asbestos:** Used before 1990 for insulation and fireproofing. Breathing fibers can cause cancer. Exposure risk during renovation/demolition. Refer to Health Canada for risks.
*   **Vermiculite insulation:** Some older vermiculite may contain asbestos. Blower door tests may not be performed. Risk if inhaled. Refer to Health Canada for information.
*   **Radon:** Naturally occurring radioactive gas. Can accumulate to high levels in enclosed spaces like houses, posing health risks. Refer to Health Canada for risks.

## Canada Greener Homes Initiative
*   **Oil to Heat Pump Affordability program:** Helps low- to median-income homeowners switch from oil heating to eligible electric heat pump systems. Details vary by province/territory.
*   **Canada Greener Homes Affordability Program:** Helps low- to median-income homeowners and tenants reduce energy bills and greenhouse gas emissions through no-cost home retrofits, delivered through provincial/territorial partners, with additional support for Indigenous organizations.
