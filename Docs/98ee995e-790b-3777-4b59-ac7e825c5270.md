# Combination Systems

## Looking to heat water and space with a single system?

Some homes simply need less heating. They may have **excellent insulation**, **low air leakage**, and make **efficient use of space**. They may also be located in an area with a milder climate.

In these types of homes, a single appliance can serve as both a **water heater** and a **space heater**, known as a **combination system**.

### System Components and Functionality

*   Typically consists of a **fan coil** installed with ductwork.
*   A small pump circulates water from the water heater through the fan coil.
*   Heated water can also be circulated through in-floor piping for **floor and radiant heating**.
*   Some combination systems may use boilers instead of water heaters.

### Energy Efficiency Considerations

*   Combination systems are **not** currently regulated under Canadas *Energy Efficiency Regulations*.
*   **Recommendation**: Look for a system rated **as a system** using the new Canadian Standards Association standard **[CSA P.9](http://shop.csa.ca/en/canada/gas-fired-domestic-and-commercial-heating-equipment-and-air-conditioning/cancsa-p9-11/invt/27032362011)***Test method for determining the performance of combined space and water heating systems (combos)*.
    *   This test method yields a **thermal performance factor** that consolidates measures for both space heating and water heating.
*   **Important Note**: If a system only has an energy factor (EF) rating for the water heater, the energy performance picture is incomplete, as the water heater performs differently in space heating mode.

### Professional Credentials

*   When purchasing a combination system, verify that your contractor or designer has the appropriate credentials.
*   For example, the Heating, Refrigeration and Air Conditioning Institute of Canada ([HRAI](http://www.hrai.ca/)) offers a certification in combination systems.
