# Canadian Heat Pump Myth Buster

*This report addresses common misconceptions about heat pumps and highlights their benefits for energy efficiency and emissions reduction in Canada.* 

## Key Facts

*   **Heat pumps** can reduce global greenhouse gas emissions by more than **500 million tonnes** by 2030.
*   On average, heat pumps operate **three times more efficiently** than electric resistance or electric boiler heating.
*   To date, over **840,000 heat pumps** have been installed in Canadian homes.

## Introduction to Heat Pumps

Buildings are the third largest source of greenhouse gas emissions in Canada, with space and water heating accounting for most of these emissions in both residential and commercial sectors. Electrifying buildings is crucial for meeting 2050 net-zero emissions targets. Heat pumps are a key part of this solution, as they efficiently use electricity to heat and cool buildings, thereby replacing fossil fuels. They function similarly to air conditioners but can reverse the process to provide heating. This dual capability offers increased comfort, often lower utility costs, and efficient cooling, which is increasingly important due to climate change.

## Types of Heat Pumps

*   **Air-Source Heat Pumps (ASHPs):** Can reduce electricity consumption for space heating by at least **50%** compared to electric resistance heating.
*   **Ground-Source Heat Pumps (GSHPs):** Operate more efficiently at lower temperatures than ASHPs because underground temperatures are more stable (warmer in winter, cooler in summer) than air temperatures.
*   **Air-to-Water Heat Pumps:** A subcategory of ASHPs that absorb heat from outside air and transfer it to a buildings hydronic circuit.
*   **Supplementary Systems:** Required in climates where temperatures drop below the heat pump's rated capacity. Examples include electric resistance built into the heat pump, electric baseboards, or an existing gas system.

## Myth Busters

### Myth: Heat pumps are a new and untested technology.

*   **Fact:** Heat pumps have a long history, with underlying technology dating back to the 18th century. Artificial refrigeration was demonstrated in 1850, and the first documented heat pump followed in 1856. They have been used in Canada since at least 1948. Modern heat pumps are distinguished by their improved ability to maintain capacity and heat in cold outdoor temperatures.

### Myth: Canada would be alone if requirements for cleaner and more efficient heating were introduced.

*   **Fact:** Many countries in Europe are eliminating fossil fuel-based heating systems. For example, Austria, France, Ireland, the Netherlands, and Norway have banned oil and gas boiler installation in new buildings by 2023. Several U.S. jurisdictions are also regulating fossil fuel-based heating systems, such as New York State prohibiting natural gas and other fossil fuel heating/cooking equipment in new buildings by 2026/2029, and San Francisco banning fossil fuels in new water heaters and furnaces.

### Myth: Canada is too cold for heat pumps.

*   **Fact:** Heat pumps are a proven technology in cold climates. Cold Climate Air Source Heat Pump (CC-ASHP) technology is specifically designed for northern climates, with the newest prototypes heating down to **31 C**. Heat pumps alone can heat more than **90%** of the time in Canadas coldest cities, and the technology continues to improve substantially.

### Myth: A heat pump is more expensive than a fossil-fuel-based heating system.

*   **Fact:** A heat pump is less expensive over its lifespan due to higher efficiency. Federal, provincial, and municipal incentive programs are also available to help lower upfront costs.
    *   Homeowners switching from an oil furnace to a CC-ASHP could save **$1000-$3500** in annual utility costs.
    *   Switching from electric resistance heating to a CC-ASHP generated **$700-1900** in savings.
    *   Savings from switching from natural gas ranged from **$50-150** per year in most Canadian regions, including the elimination of fixed natural gas charges.

### Myth: Electric grids dont have the capacity for heat pumps.

*   **Fact:** Electricity grid operators can manage the transition through strategies like increased energy efficiency, innovative timing of electricity demands, and backup fuels. Electrification will occur gradually, allowing planners to adapt. Some Canadian regions use a lot of electric heat, and heat pumps will save electricity by replacing less efficient systems. Peak demand during extreme cold can be managed with better building envelopes, smart demand-side management, and hybrid heating.

### Myth: In provinces with dirty grids, heat pumps dont lower emissions.

*   **Fact:** Heat pumps lower emissions even on grids with higher carbon intensity because of their high energy efficiency. All Canadian provinces have electricity grids clean enough for heat pumps to reduce emissions. Pairing heat pumps with other renewable energies or strategic fuel use can shift away from peak demand. With the federal government targeting net-zero emissions electricity by 2035, the emissions savings from heat pumps will continue to improve.

### Myth: Heat pumps are not climate friendly because of their refrigerants.

*   **Fact:** Even with current refrigerants, heat pumps reduce emissions by **20-80%** compared to fossil fuel heating systems. They are already a net benefit for emissions. Lower global warming potential (GWP) refrigerants are gaining traction, and building codes need to be updated to allow these into more Canadian buildings. Good maintenance, proper installation, and correct system sizing reduce refrigerant leaks.

### Myth: A gas furnace can still heat if there is an electrical power outage.

*   **Fact:** Natural gas furnaces require electricity to operate. Improved building envelopes are a better way to promote resilience during power outages, as they slow down indoor temperature drops. Smart home electrification systems can utilize electric vehicle batteries during emergencies, and heat pumps increase resilience to extreme heat through air conditioning.

### Myth: Heat pumps will not work in old homes without extensive upgrades.

*   **Fact:** Heat pumps work in almost all buildings, performing better when heat retention is optimized. They can be installed in older homes even with minimal existing insulation and air sealing, with future envelope upgrades considered during sizing. Heat pumps are an excellent choice for home retrofit projects, as many types are compatible with various existing HVAC designs. Policies and programs should aim to coordinate building envelope upgrades with heat pump installations, promoting long-term energy plans over emergency replacements.
