# ENERGY STAR Score for Multifamily Housing in Canada

## Overview

The ENERGY STAR score for Multifamily Housing assesses a propertys energy use against similar properties, considering climate, weather, and business activities. This score is derived from a statistical analysis of a peer population to identify significant drivers of energy use and normalize for these factors. The analysis results in an equation that predicts a propertys energy use based on its business activities. This prediction is then compared to the propertys actual energy use to generate a **1 to 100 percentile ranking**.

### Key Characteristics:
*   **Property Types**: Applies to commercial housing with a common entrance and interior hallways. Defined as having at least **4 storeys above ground** or a horizontal footprint of at least **600m**.
*   **Reference Data**: Based on data from the **Survey of Energy Consumption of Multi-Unit Residential Buildings (SECMURBs)**, representing energy use for the year **2018**.
*   **Adjustments**: The analysis includes adjustments for:
    *   Number of units
    *   Number of bedrooms
    *   Percentage of units located in Mid-Rise and High-Rise buildings
    *   Percent of the building that is heated
    *   Percent of the building that is cooled
    *   Weather and Climate (using heating degree days, retrieved based on postal code)
*   **Release Date**: This is the original release of the ENERGY STAR score for multifamily housing in Canada.

For more details on the methodology, refer to the *Technical Reference for the ENERGY STAR Score (PDF, 709 KB)*.

## Reference Data & Filters

The peer population data comes from the **2018 SECMURBs survey** commissioned by Natural Resources Canada (NRCan).

Four types of filters are applied to the data:
*   **Building Type Filters**
*   **Program Filters**
*   **Data Limitation Filters**
*   **Analytical Filters**

After applying all filters, the remaining dataset contains **219 observations**.

### Survey weights

The analysis uses sample weights representing the estimated number of multifamily properties in eight census metropolitan areas (Vancouver, Calgary, Winnipeg, Toronto, Ottawa-Gatineau, Hamilton, Montreal, and Halifax).

## Variables Analysed

NRCan conducted a statistical analysis to understand the key drivers of energy use in multifamily housing.

### Dependent Variables

*   **Source Energy Use Intensity (Source EUI)**: *Total source energy use divided by gross floor area, expressed in annual gigajoules per square metre (GJ/m).* This is the variable the regression equation predicts.

### Independent Variables

*Initially analysed variables* included gross floor area, cooling degree days (CDD), heating degree days (HDD), percentage of floor space heated/cooled, number of residential units, number of bedrooms, population/ownership type, common area percentage, and presence of various amenities (dishwasher hookups, laundry, fitness room, party room, EV charging stations, hot tubs).

*The final regression equation includes the following variables:*
*   **Bedroom Density**: Number of bedrooms per 100m, with a ceiling at 2.3.
*   **Unit Density**: Number of residential units per 100m.
*   **Percent Units in Mid-Rise or High-Rise**: Percentage of residential units located in mid-rise (5-9 storeys) or high-rise (10+ storeys) buildings.
*   **Percent Cooled x CDD**: Percentage of the building that is cooled multiplied by the Cooling Degree Days.
*   **Percent Heated x HDD**: Percentage of the building that is heated multiplied by the Heating Degree Days.

### Analysis of High, Mid, and Low-Rise Buildings

*   **Trend**: Shorter buildings tend to consume less energy than taller buildings.
*   **Explanation**: Low-rise buildings often have less common space (hallways, amenities) which are more energy-intensive than residential units.
*   **Variable Calculation**: The 'Percent Units in Mid-Rise or High-Rise' variable accounts for properties with mixed-height buildings. For example, a property with 60 mid-rise units and 40 low-rise units would have a value of 0.6 for this variable.

### Laundry

*   **Conclusion**: No statistically significant conclusion could be made regarding the impact of laundry on energy use due to low variability in survey data (95% of properties had laundry facilities).

### Climate Variables

*   **HDD**: Heating Degree Days were consistently significant.
*   **CDD**: Cooling Degree Days also contributed to increased energy use, though less than HDD. An adjustment factor was determined through analysis to account for cooling energy.

### Testing

*   The regression equation was further analyzed using actual data from Portfolio Manager, confirming minimal biases for fundamental operational characteristics and no regional or energy type bias.

## Regression Equation Results

*   **Dataset**: Weighted ordinary least squares regression across **219 observations**.
*   **Dependent Variable**: Source EUI.
*   **Independent Variables**: Centered relative to their weighted mean values.
*   **Significance**: All variables are significant at an 85% confidence level or better.

## Energy Star Score Lookup Table

The final regression equation predicts Source EUI based on building characteristics. The **Energy Efficiency Ratio** is calculated as:

> Energy Efficiency Ratio = Actual Source Energy Intensity / Predicted Source Energy Intensity

A ratio *lower than one* indicates greater efficiency. The efficiency ratios are sorted, and a smooth curve using a **two-parameter gamma distribution** is fitted to the data (shape parameter alpha = **10.46**, scale parameter beta = **0.0943**).

This curve is used to assign an ENERGY STAR score (1-100) based on the calculated efficiency ratio.

### 1. User enters building data into Portfolio Manager

*   Physical building information (size, location, etc.) and use details.

#### Summary of Energy Data

| Energy Data | Value          |
| :---------- | :------------- |
| Electricity | 300,000 kWh    |
| Natural gas | 218,141 m     |

#### Summary of Property Use Details

| Property Use Details                      | Value     |
| :---------------------------------------- | :-------- |
| Gross floor area (m)                     | 9,000     |
| Total Number of Units                     | 180       |
| Total Number of Bedrooms                  | 250       |
| Units  Low Rise                          | 20        |
| Units  Mid Rise                          | 0         |
| Units  High Rise                         | 160       |
| Percent That Can Be Heated                | 100%      |
| Percent That Can Be Cooled                | 100%      |
| HDD (provided by Portfolio Manager)       | 5,751     |
| CDD (provided by Portfolio Manager)       | 117       |

### 2. Portfolio Manager computes the actual source EUI

*   Total energy consumption for each fuel is converted to site energy and source energy.
*   Source energy values are summed across all fuel types.
*   Source energy is divided by gross floor area to determine actual source EUI.

#### Computing Actual Source EUI

| Fuel        | Billing Units    | Site GJ Multiplier | Site GJ | Source Multiplier | Source GJ |
| :---------- | :--------------- | :----------------- | :------ | :---------------- | :-------- |
| Electricity | 300,000 kWh      | 3.600E-03          | 1,080   | 1.83              | 1,976.4   |
| Natural gas | 218,141 m       | 3.843E-02          | 8,383   | 1.06              | 8,885.9   |
| **Total Source Energy (GJ)** |                  |                    |         |                   | **10,862**|
| **Source EUI (GJ/m)**       |                  |                    |         |                   | **1.1207**|

### 3. Portfolio Manager computes the predicted source EUI

*   Using property use details, Portfolio Manager computes each building variable value in the regression equation.
*   Centering values are subtracted to compute the centered variable.
*   Centered variables are multiplied by coefficients from the regression equation.

#### Computing Predicted Source EUI

| Variable                           | Actual Building Value | Reference Centering Value | Building Centered Variable | Coefficient | Coefficient x Centered Variable |
| :--------------------------------- | :-------------------- | :------------------------ | :------------------------- | :---------- | :------------------------------ |
| Constant                           | -                     | -                         | -                          | 1.100       | 1.100                           |
| Bedroom Density*                   | 2.300                 | 1.726                     | 0.574                      | 0.1130      | 6.52E-02                        |
| Unit Density                       | 2.000                 | 1.228                     | 0.772                      | 0.2420      | 0.187                           |
| Percent Units in Mid-Rise or High-Rise | 0.8900                | 0.7346                    | 0.1554                     | 0.2180      | 3.39E-02                        |
| Percent Cooled x CDD               | 117.0                 | 113.1                     | 3.909                      | 2.000E-04   | 7.82E-04                        |
| Percent Heated x HDD               | 5,751                 | 3,804                     | 1,947                      | 4.800E-05   | 9.35E-02                        |
| **Predicted Source EUI (GJ/m)**   |                       |                           |                            |             | **1.476**                       |

### 4. Portfolio Manager computes the energy efficiency ratio

*   Ratio = Actual Source EUI (Step 2) / Predicted Source EUI (Step 3)
*   Ratio = 1.1207 / 1.476 = **0.7593**

### 5. Portfolio Manager uses the efficiency ratio to assign a score via a lookup table

*   The ratio **0.7593** falls within the range of `>= 0.7593` and `< 0.7675` in the lookup table.
*   **The ENERGY STAR score is 76**.
