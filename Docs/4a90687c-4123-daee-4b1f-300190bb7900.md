# Deep Retrofits - Getting to Zero

## What are Net Zero and Net Zero Ready Buildings and Homes?

**Net Zero homes** produce their own energy and can supply to the power grid, while also drawing from it when necessary. Over a year, these balance out to zero energy consumption.

*   They are regular grid-tied homes that are **air-tight, well-insulated, and energy efficient.**
*   Expected to be **80% more energy efficient** than new buildings constructed to today's building code minimum.
*   Utilize **on-site (or near-site) renewable energy systems** to meet their energy needs.

## Why Go Net Zero?

### Savings

Net Zero homes offer **lowered energy and heating bills** and result in a **carbon-free home** due to their superior airtightness, insulation, and energy efficiency, balanced by renewable energy production.

### Future Proof

These homes are built to higher standards than conventional new homes, making them **more durable** with high-performance, better-insulated walls, roofs, and overall building envelopes. This ensures they are **future-proof** and built to last.

### Comfort and Health

Zero energy homes combine advanced design and superior building systems with energy efficiency and solar panels. They are **ultra-comfortable, healthy, quiet, sustainable**, and **affordable to live in.**

## Key Components of a Net Zero Home

*   **Building Envelope**: Requires high-quality, high R-value insulation and a tight building envelope. This is fundamental for the overall system's efficiency.
*   **Efficient Systems**: Incorporates high-efficiency HVAC systems (e.g., Air Source Heat Pumps) and appliances. Windows can be strategically oriented for passive heating.
*   **Renewables**: Clean, renewable energy sources are crucial for achieving net zero and reducing carbon emissions. A Net Zero Ready house is designed for easy installation of renewables.

## Financial Help and Rebates Available to Get to Zero

### Federal Programs

*   **Canada Greener Homes Loan**: Offers up to **$40,000 in interest-free loans** for terms up to 10 years for eligible retrofits.
    *   More details and application information can be found on the [Natural Resource Canada](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-efficiency/homes/canada-greener-homes-initiative/canada-greener-homes-loan/24286) site.
*   **Federal Oil to Heat Pump Affordability Program**: Provides up to **$10,000** for eligible Canadian homeowners to switch from oil heating to an energy-efficient heat pump.
    *   **Eligibility**: Home heated by oil, household income at or below median after-tax income.
    *   **Eligible Measures**: Electrical and mechanical upgrades for heat pump, ground source heat pump piping, oil tank removal, backup electrical heating, switching other oil-using systems (e.g., water heater).
*   **Canada Green Affordable Housing Program**: Funds retrofits for homeowners meeting affordability criteria. Offers up to 100% of eligible retrofit costs to a maximum of **$170,000 per unit** (forgivable loans of $85,000 per unit or 80% of eligible costs).

### Loans & Financing

*   **Clean Energy Loan (RBC Energy Saver Loan)**: For RBC customers, offers a 1% interest rate reduction or a $100 rebate on a clean energy audit when purchasing hybrid cars, ENERGY STAR appliances, or engaging in renewable energy projects.

## Steps to Net Zero

1.  **Consult with the Energy Coach**: Prepare for your first meeting by gathering information on your home's size, utility usage, heating/cooling types, number of occupants, age, and budget/cost concerns.
2.  **Set Your Goals**: Define your priorities and timeline for achieving net zero.
3.  **Book Your EnerGuide Audit**: This is crucial for financing and retrofit recommendations. If applying for the 0% interest Greener Homes Loan, the audit must be booked *before* any work begins to be covered by the loan. A list of certified professional auditors is available.
4.  **Contact Your Utility Providers**: Consult them regarding potential electrical infrastructure upgrades needed for new systems like solar or air source heat pumps (ASHP) and associated costs.
5.  **Contact Contractors**: Seek out contractors who can perform the upgrades. Energy Advisors (EAs) with specific Net Zero training can be found. CCET's Resource page lists professional organizations and contractor resources.
6.  **Determine Finance Options**: Explore available Federal and Provincial programs, loans, financing, and rebates (refer to the Financial Help page).
7.  **Begin Saving**: Initial upgrades and retrofits will immediately reduce your monthly energy costs. Contact the Energy Coach for more information.

## Greenhouse Gas Emissions

### The Challenge

*   Greenhouse gas emissions (GHG) from buildings account for **13% of Canadas total emissions**, making it the third-largest source after oil and gas production and transportation.
*   Canada is committed to a **37% emissions reduction by 2030** and achieving **net zero by 2050**.
*   The Peel region has 404,000 low-density dwellings significantly contributing to emissions. The aim is an **80% emissions reduction from these dwellings by 2050**, with Brampton and Caledon targeting all homes to be retrofitted by 2040.

### Key Statistics

*   **Peel Buildings**: Emit 40% of the total GHG emissions, and this number continues to increase.
*   **Low-rise Residential Dwellings (Peel)**: Contribute 20% of Peel's GHG emissions.
*   **Electric Heat Pumps**: Can reduce carbon emissions by 35% for the average home (Ontario Clean Air Alliance).
*   **Replacing Gas Furnaces**: Replacing a failing gas furnace with heat pump systems for space and water heating can lead to an estimated **$10,320 in lifetime total savings** (capital plus energy) (Ontario Clean Air Alliance).

## Handy at Home? Quick Fix DIY TIPS

Many simple fixes can provide immediate comfort and cost savings at little to no cost.

*   **Cold Air**: 
    *   Use inexpensive foam insulators for electrical outlets on outside walls to stop drafts.
    *   Apply weather stripping to prevent cold air entry and warm air escape.
*   **Water Heater**: 
    *   Install water heater blankets (costing $30-$40) on older models, especially if located in cold areas like basements, for significant savings.
*   **Curtains**: 
    *   Use heavy fabric curtains to reduce drafts and insulate windows.
*   **Light Bulbs**: 
    *   Switch to LED light bulbs, which last up to 100,000 hours (or 14 years) and offer significant energy savings (potentially hundreds of dollars annually).
*   **Air Filters and HVAC Maintenance**: 
    *   Regularly replace furnace filters as per manufacturer's recommendations to ensure efficient operation.
    *   Bleed hot water radiators at least once or twice a year for efficiency.
    *   Ensure oil furnaces receive annual maintenance, and natural gas/propane furnaces are serviced every two years.
    *   Avoid blocking vents and air grills with objects or furniture to maintain proper heat flow.
