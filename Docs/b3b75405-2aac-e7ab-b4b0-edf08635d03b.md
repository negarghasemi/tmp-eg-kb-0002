# Low-Solar and High-Solar Gain Glazings

A key determinant of a window's energy efficiency is its glazing. With windows accounting for more than a quarter of the annual space heating needs in a typical new home, using the most energy-efficient glazing is crucial for conserving energy and realizing cost savings.

## GlazingsNot All Created Equal

There are over 300,000 types of window products on the market, and the ideal choice of glazing can vary significantly depending on the home's age and window types. Low-emissivity (low-e) coated windows offer improved thermal performance over clear glass, but the performance of different low-e coatings in terms of solar gain varies greatly.

**Key factors for choosing glazing include:**

-   Rising cooling loads
-   Peak electric demands
-   Heat loss coefficients (**U-value or u-factor**)

One of the most important determinants of a window's energy efficiency is its **Solar Heat Gain Coefficient (SHGC)**:

-   The fraction of incident solar radiation that the window admits.
-   A number between **0 and 1**.
-   **Low solar gain products**: SHGC less than **0.30**.
-   **High solar gain products**: SHGC greater than **0.30**.

## CCHT Window Glazing Project

To assess the impact of high versus low solar gain glazing systems, a collaborative project was undertaken by the National Research Council of Canada (NRC), Natural Resources Canada (NRCan), and Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation (CMHC), with contributions from 37 companies. They utilized the [Canadian Center for Housing Technology](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy/efficiency/housing/research/5137) (CCHT) twin-house facilities for comparison.

**Testing Details:**

-   **31 glazing units** were tested.
-   **Four types of windows**: casement, fixed casement, fixed, and patio door.
-   Windows were installed on **north, south, east, and west-facing walls**.
-   Both **High Solar Gain (HSG)** and **Low Solar Gain (LSG)** windows were double-glazed and low-e coated.

**U-Factors of Tested Windows:**

-   **HSG U-Factor**: **1.76 W/mK** (0.31 Btu/hr.ftF)
-   **LSG U-Factor**: **1.62 W/mK** (0.285 Btu/hr.ftF)

## Results

Results for HSG and LSG windows were compiled for locations across North America, with findings for 10 Canadian locations indicating:

-   **HSG windows** offered **13% to 17%** energy cost savings compared to conventional windows.
-   **HSG windows** offered annual energy cost savings of **$117-$354 CDN**.
-   **LSG windows** offered **8% to 10%** energy cost savings compared to conventional windows.
-   **LSG windows** offered annual energy cost savings from **$71-$203 CDN**.

**Lessons from the field:**

-   Energy efficiency benefits depend on the mode of operation, orientation, and climate.
-   Combinations of glazing (e.g., LSG on the north, HSG on the south) can yield the best results.

## Energy Star Criteria

Using **ENERGY STAR certified products** is a straightforward way to select energy-efficient windows. To maximize passive solar gains, new specifications for ENERGY STAR in the Fall of 2010 will ensure a minimum SHGC of approximately **0.22** and a U-value of **2.0**.
