# Reaching Net-Zero in Existing Buildings

### Overview

Buildings are a significant contributor to Canada's greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions, accounting for approximately **18%** of the total. To meet its climate commitments by 2050, Canada faces the critical task of retrofitting nearly every existing building, as most structures in use today are expected to remain in service.

### Focus on Commercial Buildings

Targeting the commercial building sector, which comprises about **half a million structures**, is identified as an effective and cost-efficient strategy for emissions reduction. Commercial buildings collectively emit nearly as much as millions of residential ones.

### The Need for a Regulatory Framework

Canada requires a practical regulatory framework to drive faster and deeper reductions in energy consumption and emissions within its existing building stock. This is crucial not only for climate commitments but also for ensuring buildings continue to serve their occupants effectively for decades to come.

## Regulating Emissions in Existing Buildings

### Alterations to Existing Buildings (AEB) Code

Canada is developing its first retrofit code, the **Alterations to Existing Buildings (AEB)** code, expected for release in **2024** and provincial/territorial adoption by late **2026**. While a significant step, its requirements will be based on upgrades, repairs, or replacements initiated voluntarily by building owners, meaning it may not inherently increase retrofit demand.

### Mandatory Building Performance Standards (MBPS)

**Mandatory Building Performance Standards (MBPS)** are presented as a necessary complement to the AEB. These standards offer a tool for policymakers to *increase demand for retrofit activity, especially in the lowest-performing commercial buildings*.

#### Key Characteristics of MBPS:

*   **Performance Limits:** Set specific performance limits for large buildings.
*   **Triggering Events:** Required to be met by a specified date or upon triggering events (e.g., major renovations, building transactions).
*   **Legally Binding:** Include risks of liability and financial penalties for non-compliance.
*   **Equity-Focused:** Designed to ensure benefits are distributed equitably.

*MBPS build on voluntary measures (like benchmarking, labeling, financing, subsidies, and incentives) which have raised awareness but have been largely ineffective in achieving the necessary scale of retrofit activity.* 

## Canadian Mandatory Building Performance Standards Timeline

Canadian municipalities are emerging as leaders in regulating emissions in existing buildings. The following timeline highlights key developments:

-   **2021  City of Montreal:** Implemented **mandatory carbon reporting and disclosure** for large commercial and multi-unit residential buildings. This signals future plans for MBPS implementation.
-   **2022  City of Vancouver:** Became the **first Canadian municipality to adopt an MBPS program**. This includes mandatory energy and carbon reporting, and carbon pollution limits for commercial and retail buildings, starting in **2026**.
-   **2023  Metro Vancouver:** Indicated its intention to regulate the performance of large commercial and multi-unit residential buildings, including adopting **mandatory carbon reporting and disclosure**.
-   **2024  City of Toronto:** Will require **mandatory emissions, energy, and water reporting**, and mandatory building performance standards for large commercial buildings to low-rise residential homes.
