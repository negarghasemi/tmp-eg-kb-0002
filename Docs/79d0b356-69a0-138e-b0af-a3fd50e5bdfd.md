# HVAC & Energy Systems

*Last Modified: 2024-12-23*

## Overview

Innovative technologies for heating, ventilation, air conditioning, and refrigeration (HVACR) in Canadian homes and buildings (including commercial and recreational facilities) are crucial. These technologies aim to achieve market-feasible **net-zero energy solutions by 2030** and to **reduce electrical demand during peak times**.

## Energy Consumption Trends

Energy demand in approximately **450,000 commercial and institutional facilities** across Canada saw an increase of **over 35% between 1990 and 2004**. This growth was primarily due to increases in floor space and the use of auxiliary equipment. During the same period, the number of households in Canada grew to about **12 million dwellings**, and house sizes increased. Consequently, the total energy consumption of the housing sector rose by **10%**, amounting to an annual energy bill of approximately **$6 billion (in 2004)**.

CanmetENERGY's expertise drives efficiency gains in residential and commercial HVACR equipment and systems, leading to economic benefits and reductions in greenhouse gases for Canadians.

## Research Innovation Areas

CanmetENERGY's research focuses on areas that significantly impact overall energy consumption, including:

-   **Integrated Systems**: Development of integrated space, water, and ventilation systems.
-   **High-Efficiency Equipment**: Research into high-efficiency condensing commercial heating equipment.
-   **Micro-Cogeneration Systems**: Exploring micro-cogeneration systems with the potential for overall efficiencies and greenhouse gas (GHG) emission reductions up to **3 times lower** than conventional central electricity generation plants.

To address peak load challenges, the research targets:

-   **Demand Management**
-   **Load Shifting**: Through heat and electricity storage, advanced controls, energy cascading, and renewable energy technology integration.

Active engagement in developing **standards and regulations** ensures safe access to equipment for Canadians.
