# Keeping The Heat In - Section 8: Upgrading Windows and Exterior Doors

Windows, doors, and skylights can account for up to **35 percent of total house heat loss**.

## 8.1 Windows

There are several options for upgrading the energy efficiency of windows:

*   **Repair**: Servicing hardware like latches, cranks, and locks.
*   **Retrofit**: Applying caulking and weatherstripping, or adding glazing and storm windows.
*   **Replacement**: Total window and frame replacement with new **ENERGY STAR certified** windows or high-performance inserts. Inserts (new sash and glazing units) are an option if frames are in good condition.

### 8.1.1 Window Types

*   **Operable**: Casement, awning, hopper, tilt, turn, slider (allow ventilation/emergency escape).
*   **Non-operable (Fixed)**: Generally more airtight.
*   **Skylights**: Operable (awning-style) or fixed (flat-glazed, dome, tubular).

### 8.1.2 Material Options

Common materials include aluminum, fiberglass, vinyl, wood, or combinations. The overall energy efficiency is primarily dependent on the **product's design**, reflected in its energy-performance rating, rather than just the material.

### 8.1.3 Glazing Options

*   **Glazing Layers**: Single, double, triple, or quad panes. More layers reduce outdoor noise.
    *   *Note: One pane in triple-glazed may be polyester film to reduce weight.*
*   **Low-E Coatings**: Transparent metal coating on glazing surfaces.
    *   **Benefit**: Reduce heat loss in winter and heat gain in summer by up to 50%, maintaining comfortable temperatures, reducing HVAC load, and saving energy.
    *   **Passive Low-E**: High Solar Heat Gain Coefficient (SHGC) to harness sun's energy for heating.
    *   **Solar Control Low-E**: Lower SHGC to reduce cooling load.
    *   **After-market**: Low-E films can be applied to existing units.
*   **Gas Fill**: Odourless, colourless, inert gas (argon or krypton) between glazings to reduce heat transfer.
*   **Insulated Spacers**: Thermal breaks using foam, plastic, or composites reduce heat loss and condensation along edges.
*   **Optimal Glazing Spacing**: 12 to 16 mm (1/2 to 5/8 in.) to minimize convection currents and heat loss.

### 8.1.4 Rating Information

Key metrics for windows and skylights in Canada:

*   **U-factor**: Measures rate of heat loss. **Lower values indicate greater resistance to heat loss**.
    *   **Performance**: Better double-glazed windows:  1.2 W/mK. Triple-glazed windows: as low as 0.8 W/mK.
*   **Solar Heat Gain Coefficient (SHGC)**: Fraction of solar heat passing through (0 to 1).
    *   **Lower SHGC**: Less solar heat transmitted, reduces cooling load.
    *   **Higher SHGC**: More solar energy allowed/trapped, reduces heating load.
    *   **Optimal for renovated/new homes in Canada**: 0.3-0.4.
    *   **High SHGC (>0.4) suitable for**: Homes built before 1980 or not well insulated, sunny areas with short cooling seasons (<2 months, <25C), passive solar heating designs.
*   **Energy Rating (ER)**: Unitless number reflecting balance between U-factor and solar gain. **Higher numbers indicate better performance**.
*   **Visible Transmittance (VT)**: Percentage of visible light passing through. **Higher numbers mean more light**.
*   **Centre-of-Glass U-factor**: Only considers the glass portion, not the entire window assembly; can be misleadingly high.

### 8.1.5 Replacing Glazing, Sashes, and Windows

Energy-efficient windows make homes more comfortable by reducing drafts and increasing interior window surface temperature, reducing condensation. Features of energy-efficient windows include:

*   **Double-, triple- or quadruple-glazing**
*   **Low-emissivity (low-E) glass**
*   **Inert gas** (argon or krypton) in sealed unit
*   **Low conductivity or warm-edge spacer bars**
*   **Insulated frames and sashes**
*   **Good air tightness**

If glazing is single pane or in poor condition, consider a new sealed glazing window insert. If the frame is poor, replace the entire unit.

### 8.1.6 Taking Stock (Inspection)

*   **Check for damage**: Rot, mold, staining on/around the window.
*   **Inspect**: Condition of glass, putty, paint, weatherstripping, and hardware.
*   **Assess air leakage**: Around the frame and at all movable joints. Use a leak detector.

### 8.1.7 Condensation Problems

*   **Cause**: New, tighter windows can increase indoor humidity if ventilation is inadequate, leading to condensation.
*   **Solutions**: Reduce indoor humidity, improve house ventilation, or install more efficient windows (e.g., ENERGY STAR certified, triple-glazed).
*   **Between panes (sealed units)**: If the seal is broken, replace the glazing unit. *Consider warranty. Temporary fixes are less effective.*
*   **Between panes (non-sealed/storm windows)**: Moister interior air leaking past the inner pane. **Weatherstrip the inner sash more tightly than the outer**; ensure weep holes on storm windows are open.

### 8.1.8 Interior Caulking

*   **Application**: Continuous bead around window trim where it meets the wall, at mitered joints, and between trim and frame.
*   **Material**: Use caulk intended for indoor use, paintable, and good quality.
*   **Gaps**: For small gaps ( 6 mm /  in.), insulate and caulk. For larger gaps, use a backer rod with caulk or low-expansion foam.
*   **Additional sealing**: Red technical tape can cover the joint between the wall and window frame (ensure it's hidden by trim).

### 8.1.9 Exterior Caulking

*   **Role**: Last defense against rain. Should be applied **only after interior sealing**.
*   **Primary defense**: Proper flashing and a detailed drainage plane are more crucial.
*   **Caution**: Caulking exterior first can trap moist air in the wall, causing damage.

### 8.1.10 Weatherstripping

*   **Purpose**: Create a seal between frame and door/window sash to block rain, dust, and minimize air leakage.
*   **Types**: Compression, brush, sweep, magnetic. Compression/magnetic seals are typically most airtight (casement, awning, hopper, tilt-turn windows, operable skylights).
*   **Installation Steps**:
    1.  Adjust and square misaligned windows.
    2.  Remove old weatherstripping, caulk, and paint blobs.
    3.  Clean contact surfaces.
    4.  Apply new weatherstripping (reinforce adhesive types with staples for frequently used openings).
    5.  Check for smooth operation of hinges, slides, and hardware.
    6.  Periodically check for wear.
*   **Building Code**: Every bedroom must have at least one operable window for emergency escape.
*   **Storm Windows**: Seal the inside window more tightly than the outside window to reduce condensation.

#### Specifics for Window Types:

*   **Double-hung and Single-hung**: Weatherstrip sides, top, and bottom of moving sash (e.g., V-type weatherstripping).
*   **Sliding Windows**: Typically use brush weatherstripping, installed in slots. Sashless sliders are inefficient and should be supplemented with interior storm windows or replaced.
*   **Swing-type (Hinged) Windows**: Treat like doors. Apply weatherstripping to the frame or stop to meet the sash, creating a pressure seal. Often requires a combination of V-strip and compression-type weatherstripping.

### 8.1.11 Storm Window Systems

Storm windows or double-glazed sealed units can **reduce heat loss by almost half**.

*   **Exterior Storm Window Systems**: Seasonal or permanent. The main interior window must be air-sealed more tightly than the storm window to prevent moisture issues.
*   **Interior Storm Window Systems**: Generally attached directly to the window frame. Lighter and more accessible, especially for upper floors. Should be sealed tightly. Can also help keep heat out in summer.
    *   **Heat-shrink film with double-sided tape**: Inexpensive, temporary, excellent seal. Window cannot be opened once installed. Tape may lift paint upon removal.
    *   **Clear rigid acrylic sheets with magnetic seals**: More durable and expensive. Requires proper storage when not in use.

#### Specialty Products

*   **Applied Window Films (Solar-Control Coating)**: Reduce solar gain, protect furnishings. **Caution**: Can reduce beneficial solar gain in winter, may increase thermal stress on glass (risk of breakage), and might void manufacturer warranties. Do not use on all windows.
*   **Window Coverings**: Curtains, blinds, shutters, awnings, and trees can reduce radiant heat loss in winter and solar gain in summer. Insulated drapes offer more benefit. Non-airtight coverings can cause winter condensation.

### 8.1.12 Skylights

*   **Types**: Flat glazed, dome lens, tubular. Should have a low curb.
*   **Maintenance**: Ensure hardware works, seals are in good order. Repair exterior seals and flashings to prevent rain entry.
*   **Insulation**: Curbs and interior tunnels must be well insulated and air-sealed to reduce condensation.
*   **Overheating**: Reduce summer overheating with light-reflective glazing and blinds.
*   **Operation**: Avoid opening skylights (or upper-storey windows) in hot weather if indoor air is cooler, as this can cause a reverse stack effect, drawing hot air in and negating cooling efforts.

## 8.2 Doors

Doors come in swinging (hinged) and sliding styles. They can be solid or have glazing. **Insulated core doors are more thermally resistant** than solid material doors or those with glazing.

*   **Issues**: Poor installation, wear, shifting foundations, and seasonal warping can cause doors to become out of square.
*   **Remedy**: Fix or replace the door, frame, hardware, gaskets, and weatherstripping. The same preparation and repair techniques as for windows apply.

### 8.2.1 Weatherstripping the Doors

*   **Top and Sides**: Good quality V-shaped vinyl weatherstripping is effective, accommodating seasonal warping.
*   **Additional Protection**: Attach weatherstripping to the stop to press against the door face (many combination metal/foam/rubber types available).
*   **Door Sill/Bottom**: A difficult area to seal but crucial. Use durable, flexible gasketed door-bottom weatherstripping (attached to the door) or full/partial threshold weatherstripping (attached to the sill).
*   **Sliding Patio Doors**: Replace worn weatherstripping and hardware. Consider replacing older, inefficient units with **ENERGY STAR certified** ones. Temporarily seal unused doors in winter with removable sealant or heat-shrink film.

## 8.3 Contracting the Work

*   **Contractor Selection**: Air sealing and window/door repairs can be part of an air sealing contractor's work. When getting estimates, ensure the contractor has experience and ask about third-party warranties.
*   **Professional Installation**: Essential for warranty coverage, proper operation, and preventing drafts or water leakage.
*   **Installation Criteria**:
    *   Provide an **airtight, insulated seal**.
    *   Window/door must retain its original shape, be level and square (unless designed for an angle).
    *   Should not support any load other than its own weight (unless specified).
    *   Exterior finishing (e.g., flashing) must prevent water penetration by directing water away from openings.
    *   Appropriate sealing tapes and caulking should be used as recommended by the manufacturer.
    *   Skylight or tunnel must be well sealed (interior/exterior) and insulated.
*   **Types of Installation**:
    *   **Insert Retrofit**: New window/door installed into the existing frame. Less expensive, less disturbance. Only suitable if the existing frame is in good condition, properly sealed, and insulated. (Not typical for skylights).
    *   **Complete Tear Out**: Removal of the old unit and its frame. Allows for an airtight insulated seal between the new product and the rough opening. Often retains or enlarges the original opening size.

*   **Resources**: For certified window installations, refer to the [Window Wise program](https://sawdac.com/Window-Wise.htm).
