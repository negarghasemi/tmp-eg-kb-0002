# Computer Room Air Conditioners: Energy Efficiency Regulations

**Date of Publication**: May 2024

## Overview
Natural Resources Canada (NRCan) is initiating a consultation process to introduce Computer Room Air Conditioners (CRACs) as a new energy-using product under Canada's Energy Efficiency Regulations. The goal is to harmonize energy efficiency and testing standards with those in the United States.

- **Webinar**: Tuesday, June 11, 2024, 1:00-2:00 pm EDT
- **Regulatory Body**: Natural Resources Canada (NRCan)
- **Harmonization Partner**: United States Department of Energy (U.S. DOE)
- **Next Steps**: A regulatory proposal will be pre-published in the *Canada Gazette*, Part I, in 2025.

## Background
CRACs are not currently regulated as energy-using products in Canada. The proposed amendments aim to save energy, reduce energy costs, decrease greenhouse gas emissions, and encourage efficient product designs. This initiative aligns with the *Memorandum of Understanding Between the Department of Natural Resources of Canada and the Department of Energy of the United States of America Concerning Cooperation on Energy* (2021).

## Product Description
CRACs are defined consistently with AHRI standards 1360-2022 and the U.S. DOE. They are commercial packaged or split system air-conditioners used in computer rooms or similar applications, utilizing dedicated compressors and refrigerant cooling coils rather than chilled-water coils.

### Definitions
- **Computer Room Air Conditioner (CRAC)**: A computer and data processing room air conditioner that uses dedicated compressors and refrigerant cooling coils.
- **Air-cooled CRAC**: A CRAC refrigeration system with an air-cooled condenser heat exchanger.
- **Glycol-cooled CRAC**: A CRAC refrigeration system with a glycol-cooled condenser heat exchanger.
- **Water-cooled CRAC**: A CRAC refrigeration system with a water-cooled condenser heat exchanger.
- **Computer and Data Processing Room Air Conditioner (CDPR)**: An air conditioning unit specifically marketed for cooling data centers and information technology equipment (ITE) with one or more factory-made assemblies, including an evaporator or chilled water-cooling coil, air-moving, and air-filtering devices.
- **Ceiling-mounted CDPR**: A CDPR indoor unit designed for installation above a dropped ceiling, with one airflow direction (horizontal) and multiple airflow connections.
- **Floor-mounted CDPR**: A CDPR indoor unit designed for installation on a raised floor, floor stand, or solid floor, with two airflow configuration options (direction and connection).
    - **Flow direction of the floor-mounted CDPR**:
        - **Downflow**: Return air enters above the cooling coil, discharge air leaves below.
        - **Horizontal-flow**: Neither downflow nor upflow.
        - **Upflow**: Return air enters below the cooling coil, discharge air leaves above.

*NRCan is not considering regulating **computer room air handlers**, which use chilled-water coils for cooling.*

## Standards

### Testing Standard
NRCan is considering incorporating by reference the following U.S. testing standard:
- **Appendix E to Subpart F of Part 431** to the U.S. Code of Federal Regulations, entitled *Uniform Test Method for Measuring the Energy Consumption of Computer Room Air Conditioners*.

## Important Dates
- **In-force Date**: Modifications to the Regulations would come into force six months after the date of publication in the *Canada Gazette*, Part II.
- **Application Date**: New standards would apply to CRACs manufactured on or after **May 28, 2024**.

### Regulatory Pathway
- NRCan is considering amending the Regulations through a Governor in Council amendment.
- Changes would be pre-published in the *Canada Gazette*, Part I, followed by a public comment period.
- Final publication in the *Canada Gazette*, Part II, depends on public comments, typically within a year of the initial public comment period.

## Labelling and Verification
- **Labelling Requirements**: NRCan is *not* considering specific labelling requirements for CRACs.
- **Verification Requirements**: Products must carry a **third-party verification mark** from a Standards Council of Canada (SCC) accredited certification body, indicating compliance with energy efficiency standards and verified reporting information.

## Reporting Requirements

### Energy Efficiency Report
For CRACs, the energy efficiency report must include the following information. This report must be submitted by the dealer to NRCan **before** the product is imported into Canada or traded inter-provincially for the first time.

- **Mount & Flow**: Floor mounted (downflow, upflow ducted, upflow non-ducted, horizontal flow) or ceiling mounted (ducted, non-ducted).
- **Equipment type**:
    - Air-cooled
    - Air-cooled with fluid economizer
    - Air-cooled with free air discharge condenser
    - Air-cooled with free air discharge condenser and fluid economizer
    - Air-cooled with ducted condenser
    - Air-cooled with ducted condenser and fluid economizer
    - Water-cooled
    - Water-cooled with fluid economizer
    - Glycol-cooled
    - Glycol-cooled with fluid economizer
- **Net sensible cooling capacity**
- **Flow**: Downflow, upflow ducted, upflow non-ducted, horizontal flow, ducted, non-ducted
- **NSenCOP efficiency**
- **Brand name**
- **Model number**
- **Name of the manufacturer**
- **Name of the certification body** whose verification mark would be affixed to the product

### Import Report
A dealer importing these products into Canada must include the following information on the customs release document:

- **Name of product**
- **Model number**
- **Brand name**
- **Address of the dealer** importing the product
- **Purpose for which the product is being imported** (e.g., for sale/lease in Canada without modification, for sale/lease after modification to comply, or for use as a component in an exported product).
