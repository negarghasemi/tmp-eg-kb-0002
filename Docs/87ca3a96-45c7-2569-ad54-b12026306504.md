# Guide to ENERGY STAR Certified Homes

## Whats in an ENERGY STAR certified home?

### Common features
ENERGY STAR certified homes include:
- **Energy-efficient windows**
- An **HRV** (Heat Recovery Ventilator) to recapture lost heat and provide outdoor air
- **400 kWh/year of electrical savings**, typically obtained through ENERGY STAR certified lighting or appliances
- Must meet certain **thresholds for airtightness**

### Flexible features
ENERGY STAR certified homes also feature elements that can differ from home to home, such as:
- **Level of insulation**
- **Efficiency of the water heater**
- **Extent of air tightness**

The specific upgrades depend on the builders design and construction choices, making each home unique in its energy efficiency elements.

## The ENERGY STAR label

### What does the ENERGY STAR Standard specify?
- Developed by Natural Resources Canada (NRCan), the **ENERGY STAR for New Homes Standard (ESNH)** outlines the technical requirements for a home to receive the ENERGY STAR label.
- The main objective is for a home to be, on average, approximately **20% more efficient** than one built to local building codes, specifically concerning space and water heating.
- The Standard is updated as regional building codes change. During transition periods, some homes might be 20% more efficient than the *previous* building code, rather than the *current* one.

### How do builders meet the Standard?
Builders have two paths to certify their homes:

1.  **Prescriptive path**:
    - Builders construct homes based on **predetermined specifications** outlined in the Standard.
    - They must meet a set of **minimum requirements**.
    - They then choose from a series of **upgrade options**, each allocated points.
    - The chosen features must accumulate a specific number of points, determined by the province and climate zone.

2.  **Performance path**:
    - Builders aim to meet a specific **energy performance target** for their province and climate zone.
    - They must still meet **minimum requirements** but have high flexibility in choosing how to reach the energy target.
    - The home is evaluated using **energy simulation software** to ensure it meets the required target.

*Both paths are equally valid and require on-site verification by an energy advisor.*

### Who are the different parties involved?

-   **Natural Resources Canada (NRCan)**:
    -   Administrator of the program and the ENERGY STAR mark at the national level.
    -   Responsible for developing and updating the ENERGY STAR for New Homes Standard.

-   **Service organizations and energy advisors**:
    -   Licensed to use ENERGY STAR trademarks and tools.
    -   Maintain a network of energy advisors who verify homes meet the Standard.
    -   *Operate as independent businesses; they are not agents, partners, or employees of NRCan.*

-   **Builders**:
    -   Licensed to use ENERGY STAR trademarks.
    -   Work with service organizations/energy advisors to construct certified homes.
    -   *NRCan does not endorse any builder, energy advisor, or specific product, and accepts no liability in their selection or performance.*
    -   *NRCan is not responsible for resolving disputes or enforcing external contracts between homeowners and third parties.*

*NRCan takes misuse of the ENERGY STAR name and symbol seriously and may terminate participant agreements for non-compliance.*

### Im ready to buy, what are some questions to ask my builder?

-   Which **energy efficiency features** will be included in my ENERGY STAR certified home (e.g., insulation R-values, equipment efficiency, energy-efficient lighting or products)?
-   What should I do to **maintain and operate** my ENERGY STAR certified home?
-   To which **version of the ENERGY STAR Standard** will my home be built? Is it based on the current or previous building code?

### I have questions about my Energy Star home. Who can I contact?

1.  **First contact**: The **ENERGY STAR for New Homes service organization** that oversaw the verification and labeling of your home. Their contact information can be found on your ENERGY STAR for New Homes label or Certificate.
2.  **If unresolved**: You may contact NRCan to initiate a certification review process.
    -   Complete and submit the [ENERGY STAR for New Homes Certification Review Form](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/19gxwXaxW6gmOzik1mf5dURZRJIw8C-yx?usp=sharing).
    -   Clearly outline issues, actions taken, and include supporting documentation (photos, emails).
    -   Email completed forms to: **esnhprogram-programmeesmn@nrcan-rncan.gc.ca**

*NRCan is not responsible for resolving disputes or enforcing external contracts.*
