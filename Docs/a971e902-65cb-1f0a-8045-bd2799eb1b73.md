# Battery Chargers (Uninterruptible Power Supplies)

**Date:** May 2024

## Technical Bulletin on Amending the Standards

*Webinar: Tuesday, May 28, 1:00-2:00 pm EDT*

Natural Resources Canada (NRCan) is updating its Forward Regulatory Plan to amend Canadas *Energy Efficiency Regulations* to introduce testing and energy efficiency standards for Uninterruptible Power Supplies (UPS). This technical bulletin initiates the consultation process to collect stakeholder views on the proposed requirements.

Input from this stage will inform the development of future amendments. NRCan will conduct a cost-benefit analysis using Canadian market data to assess economic and environmental impacts. The Government of Canada intends to pre-publish a regulatory proposal in the *Canada Gazette*, Part I, in 2025.

## Background

Currently, UPS are not defined as an energy-using product under the Canadian Regulations. NRCan aims to harmonize with United States Department of Energy (U.S. DOE) standards for UPS, following the U.S. DOE's final rule on January 10, 2020, which expanded the scope of battery chargers to include UPS and introduced new energy efficiency and testing standards. Harmonization efforts align with the *Memorandum of Understanding Between the Department of Natural Resources of Canada and the Department of Energy of the United States of America Concerning Cooperation on Energy*.

## Product Description

A **UPS** is defined as a battery charger consisting of convertors, switches, and an energy storage system for maintaining load power during input power failure. It is being considered for inclusion within the scope and definition of a battery charger.

For the purpose of this bulletin, a UPS utilizes a NEMA 1-15P or NEMA 5-15P input plug and includes:

*   **Voltage and Frequency Dependent UPS (VFD UPS)**: Produces AC output where voltage and frequency depend on input. Does not provide corrective functions.
*   **Voltage Independent UPS (VI UPS)**: Produces AC output within a specific tolerance band, independent of input under-voltage or over-voltage variations, without depleting stored energy. Output frequency depends on input frequency.
*   **Voltage and Frequency Independent UPS (VFI UPS)**: Maintains AC output voltage and frequency independent of input variations, protecting the load without depleting stored energy.

*NRCan is considering harmonizing with the U.S. definitions of UPS types, including back-up battery chargers.*

### Testing Standard

NRCan is considering harmonizing with the U.S. testing standard through an ambulatory incorporation by reference to:

*   **Appendix Y1 to Subpart B, Part 430 of Title 10** to the U.S. Code of Federal Regulations, entitled *Uniform Test Method for Measuring the Energy Consumption of Battery Chargers*.

### Energy Efficiency Standard

NRCan is considering harmonizing with U.S. DOE energy efficiency standards for UPS through an ambulatory incorporation by reference to an NRCan technical standards document that would reproduce:

*   **Paragraph 430.32(z)(3) of Subpart C, Part 430 of Title 10** to the United States Code of Federal Regulations, as amended from time to time.

The energy efficiency standards under consideration for UPS are summarized below:

| Product Class | Nominal (rated) output power (P_rated) | Energy Efficiency Standards (minimum efficiency) |
| :------------ | :------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------- |
| VFD           | 0 W < P_rated  300 W                  | (1.20E-6 * P_rated) + (7.17E-04 * P_rated) + 0.862 |
| VFD           | 300 W < P_rated  700 W                | (7.85E-8 * P_rated) + (1.01E-04 * P_rated) + 0.946 |
| VFD           | P_rated > 700 W                        | (7.23E-9 * P_rated) + (7.52E-06 * P_rated) + 0.977 |
| VI            | 0 W < P_rated  300 W                  | (1.20E-8 * P_rated) + (7.19E-04 * P_rated) + 0.863 |
| VI            | 300 W < P_rated  700 W                | (7.67E-8 * P_rated) + (1.05E-04 * P_rated) + 0.947 |
| VI            | P_rated > 700 W                        | (4.62E-9 * P_rated) + (8.54E-06 * P_rated) + 0.979 |
| VFI           | 0 W < P_rated  300 W                  | (3.13E-8 * P_rated) + (1.96E-04 * P_rated) + 0.543 |
| VFI           | 300 W < P_rated  700 W                | (2.60E-8 * P_rated) + (3.65E-04 * P_rated) + 0.764 |
| VFI           | P_rated > 700 W                        | (1.70E-8 * P_rated) + (3.85E-06 * P_rated) + 0.876 |

## Important Dates

*   **Effective Date**: Modifications would come into force **six months** after publication in the *Canada Gazette*, Part II.
*   **Regulatory Pathway**: Governor in Council amendment, with pre-publication in the *Canada Gazette*, Part I, in 2025, followed by a public comment period.

## Labelling Requirements

NRCan is **not considering labelling requirements** for UPS.

## Verification Requirements

*   Products must carry a **third-party verification mark** from a Standards Council of Canada accredited certification body, indicating compliance with energy efficiency standards and verified reporting information.

## Reporting Requirements

### Energy Efficiency Report

For UPS, the energy efficiency report would include:

*   **Name of product**: uninterruptible power supply
*   **Brand name**
*   **Model number**
*   **Name of the manufacturer**
*   **Name of the certification body** whose verification mark would be affixed to the product
*   **Whether a mathematical model was used** to generate any of the product-specific information
*   **Product class** (e.g., VFD UPS)
*   **Nominal active power**, in watts (W)
*   **Nominal apparent power**, in volt-amperes (VA)
*   **Nominal input voltage**, in volts (V)
*   **Nominal output voltage**, in volts (V)
*   **Efficiency at 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% of reference test load**
*   **Average load adjusted efficiency** (of the lowest and highest input dependency modes)

*This report must be submitted by the dealer to NRCan before the product is imported into Canada or traded inter-provincially for the first time.*

### Import Report

A dealer importing these products into Canada would include the following information on the customs release document:

*   **Name of product**
*   **Model number**
*   **Brand name**
*   **Address of the dealer importing the product**
*   **Purpose for which the product is being imported** (e.g., for sale or lease in Canada without modification; for sale or lease in Canada after modification to comply with energy efficiency standards; or for use as a component in a product being exported from Canada)
