# Grants for Canadian Homeowners in the North and Off-Grid Communities

**Status:** Closed: Canada Greener Homes Grant. December 31, 2025, was the last day to submit documents. Requests submitted by the deadline will continue to be processed.

## Program Overview
Canadians in northern and off-grid communities faced specific challenges with home retrofits, including higher equipment and labor costs. To address this, all retrofit amounts were increased by an additional 30% for these communities.

In addition to the eligible retrofits available for all Canadians, homeowners in northern and off-grid communities were eligible for replacements of existing fossil-fuel burning equipment. Homeowners in a northern community were also eligible for additional insulation measures (attic, ceiling, and exposed floor insulation).

### The Canada Greener Homes Initiative offered:
-   **Grants from $162.50 to $5,000** for eligible and recommended home retrofits.
-   **Up to $600** as a maximum contribution toward the total costs of pre- and post-retrofit EnerGuide evaluations, if the grant process (including at least one eligible retrofit) was completed.
-   **Interest-free loans of up to $40,000**, with a repayment term of 10 years, to help undertake major home retrofits.

The maximum grant amount a household could receive was **$5,600**. This included up to **$5,000** for all eligible retrofits combined and up to **$600** for the pre- and post-retrofit evaluations combined.

_**Note:** If you live in Ontario, Quebec, or Nova Scotia, please refer to their specific webpages for information._

## Ineligible Retrofits
The Canada Greener Homes Initiative focuses on improving the energy efficiency of *existing* homes. Additions to existing homes were not eligible. Renovations as part of a rebuild might also be ineligible depending on the extent of the rebuild. It was highly recommended to contact the program for advice before starting renovations.

**Retrofits that were not eligible:**
-   Retrofits done before a pre-retrofit evaluation.
-   Retrofits done before December 1, 2020.
-   Products installed in new home additions built between the pre- and post-retrofit EnerGuide evaluations:
    -   Home insulation: _The portion of the new areas did not qualify (e.g., wall insulation, attic insulation) and may reduce the total eligible amount._
    -   Windows and doors: _New windows and doors in new home additions did not qualify._
    -   Components requiring 100% upgrades did not qualify (e.g., crawl space wall and header insulation, exposed floor insulation).
-   Leased or rented equipment.
-   Air conditioners.
-   Tankless or storage water heaters other than heat pump water heaters.
-   Skylights.
-   Roofs.
-   Windows and doors in a garage.

### <a id=\"s5\"></a>Space and water heating
Furnaces and boilers were eligible only for homeowners in northern and off-grid communities. Space heating accounts for over 60% of energy use in Canadian homes. Switching to energy-efficient heating equipment like heat pumps can save energy and reduce utility bills and carbon footprint.

#### What is a heat pump?
A heat pump transfers energy from the environment (air or ground) for heating or cooling. It uses electrical energy to move thermal energy, offering higher energy efficiency than converting other energy sources directly. Cold-climate heat pumps are designed for Canadian winters.

#### Why install a heat pump?
Heat pumps can provide year-round heating/cooling. While purchase costs can be higher, energy cost savings can recoup this investment. Consult your energy advisor to determine if a heat pump is right for you.

### <a id=\"s6\"></a>Renewable energy systems
Renewable energy is derived from natural processes that replenish faster than they are consumed, such as solar energy.

#### Solar photovoltaic (PV) system (including solar panels and inverters)
-   **Eligibility:**
    -   All equipment must be purchased in Canada (online from Canadian distributors).
    -   System must include one or more PV panels and inverter(s) certified to CSA Standards.
    -   Total system peak power capacity must be  1.0 kW DC.
    -   For grid-connected systems, a copy of the letter of approval for interconnection is required.
    -   Solar panels must be permanently mounted (on house or ground) on the property.
-   **Additional Information:**
    -   No national/provincial/territorial certifications for solar PV designers/installers. Self-installation is allowed, but personal labor costs are not eligible.
    -   Eligible for first-time installation or addition to existing system.
    -   Must be designed and installed according to local building and electrical requirements.
    -   Recommended to have a qualified solar PV professional assess your home as \"Solar Ready\" before pre-retrofit EnerGuide evaluation.
    -   For roof-top installations (grid-connected and off-grid), CSA SPE-900-13 guideline is recommended.
    -   Building permits may be required.
    -   _If batteries are purchased for storing solar energy, a portion of the cost may be eligible for climate resiliency grants._

### <a id=\"s7\"></a>Resiliency measures
Extreme weather can damage homes. Adding climate resiliency measures can protect against fire, flooding, wind, and power loss. These measures must be combined with an energy efficiency retrofit.

#### Batteries connected to solar photovoltaic (PV) systems
-   **Eligibility:**
    -   Battery system must be connected to a permanently mounted photovoltaic system.
    -   Batteries can be for new systems, replacements, or supplements to existing systems.
    -   Batteries must be rated for deep cycle (any technology).
    -   Inverter and charge controller purchase/installation costs can be included.
    -   Batteries must be permanently installed. Portable batteries and electric vehicles are not eligible.
-   **Best practices:**
    -   Consider an inverter with true sinewave output, min. 1200 W continuous, min. 2500 W surge capability.
    -   Consider a battery system with min. 4500 Watt hours total capacity at 20 hours.
    -   Inverter and charge controller should be certified to CSA C22.2 107.1 or CSA C22.2 62109.
    -   Discuss maintenance, warranties, and specifications with supplier/contractor.
    -   Installation or inspection must be by a licensed and trained professional.
    -   An electrical permit may be required. Check local codes.

#### Basement Wall Waterproofing
-   **Eligibility:**
    -   Waterproofing must be performed on the exterior side of below-grade basement walls with rubberized or polymer membranes (e.g., waterproof-rated spray, trowel-on, roll-on, sheet materials).
    -   Must use a waterproofing membrane, not a damp/moisture-proofing membrane.
    -   Minimum 80% of the below-grade wall area must be waterproofed (exterior-facing walls only, not party walls).
    -   Crawl space and slab-on-grade foundations are not eligible.
-   **Best practices:**
    -   Waterproofing 100% of the below-grade wall area.
    -   Seal all existing cracks, holes, and penetrations with hydraulic sealing compounds.
    -   Ensure proper drainage (e.g., air gap drainage membrane, drainage board, free draining backfill).
    -   Ensure drainage tile (weeping tile or French drain) is present and in good working condition.
    -   Install exterior drainage membranes.
    -   Add insulation to the exterior side of the wall when waterproofing.
    -   Verify products are specified for waterproofing. Simple paint brush or roller applied interior sealants are not eligible.
    -   Review warranties from waterproofing companies.
    -   DIY waterproofing is acceptable but requires evidence (pictures, invoices).
    -   Obtain a building permit if required.

#### Moisture Proofing Crawl Space Floor, Walls and Headers
All closed and vented crawl spaces are eligible for this grant (also known as encapsulation).
-   **Eligibility:**
    -   100% of the floors, walls, and headers of all crawl spaces must be covered by a continuous moisture barrier (e.g., minimum 6 mil polyethylene barrier on floor, closed-cell foam on walls/headers). Consult local building authority.
    -   All seams, edges, and penetrations must be sealed with suitable materials (e.g., tape, caulking).
-   **Best practices:**
    -   Address all water infiltration and pest issues first.
    -   If a crawl space is attached to a basement, the crawl spaces moisture barrier must be sealed to the basement foundation junction.
    -   Continuous moisture barrier should be 0.75 perms or less, or as specified by local building code.
    -   Use 10 mil or thicker polyethylene for durability.
    -   If wall/header insulation (e.g., closed-cell foam) is used as the moisture barrier, it may also be eligible for the crawl space insulation grant.
    -   Encapsulation can be performed by homeowner or contractor.
    -   Review warranties.
    -   If a combustion appliance is in the crawl space, verify with a heating professional.
    -   Obtain a building permit if required.

| Option | Grant amount |

| Moisture proofing of 100% of crawl space floor, walls and headers | $780 |

### <a id=\"s8\"></a>Multi-unit Residential Buildings (MURBs)
A MURB contains two or more units.
-   **Eligibility requirements for MURBs:**
    -   Three or fewer storeys above ground.
    -   Building area does not exceed 600 m.
    -   Between 2 and 100 units.
    -   Units are fully or partially stacked (up/down) or joined by a common space (side-by-side for secondary suites).
    -   At least 50% of total floor area must be residential living space.
    -   Habitable year-round.

_The Canada Greener Homes Initiative is based on the EnerGuide Rating System, limited to houses and low-rise MURBs governed by Part 9 of the National Building Code of Canada. High-rise condos are outside this scope._

-   **Eligible Retrofits for all MURBs:**
    -   Insulation
    -   Air sealing
    -   Windows and doors
    -   Renewable energy
    -   Resiliency measures
-   **Two-unit MURBs (including houses with secondary suites)** were entitled to the same grants as attached and detached houses (e.g., heat pumps, heat pump water heaters).
-   **MURBs (excluding two-unit MURBs and houses with secondary suites)** were **not eligible** for grants related to:
    -   Heat pumps and heat pump water heaters
    -   Thermostats
    -   Furnaces and boilers (in northern and off-grid communities)

_Townhomes and row houses connected side-by-side or back-to-back are not MURBs and were eligible as individual houses._
_Retirement homes are not MURBs and are **not eligible**._
